# Ensembles of states that share rigid segments: hinges, reflections and interval moments

**Geometric Inference division — third note (2026-11-26).**  Labels as in
`ENSEMBLE_RIGIDITY.md` ("the report").  Unrefereed; written in answer to the Science
division's SCI-RES-0029 §3, whose statements (a one-torsion pair is never identified; the
conic condition fails for at most three differing torsions; what the data see of a torsion
distribution) are the starting point and are credited where used.

## 0. Summary

The theorems of the report are about states that differ everywhere.  The commonest motion
is the opposite: two states that differ in a few torsions ("hinges") and share the rigid
segments between them.  There the conic condition (CC) fails (Science division), and the
question was whether identifiability fails with it.

1. **Reduction (Proposition M2).**  If every shared segment has its constrained edge
   directions on no conic (five atoms or more, generic), then every ensemble with the
   averaged squared distances of a hinge ensemble consists of *assemblies of the same rigid
   segments* — each consecutive pair related by an element of `O(2)`: a rotation about the
   hinge bond **or a reflection in a plane through it**.  The reflected assemblies are valid
   conformations and cannot be left out.
2. **What the data are (Lemma M1, Proposition M2).**  The averaged squared distances of an
   ensemble on such a family are equivalent to its **interval moments**
   `𝔼[g_p g_{p+1} ⋯ g_q]`, the mean `3 × 3` matrices of the products of consecutive hinge
   transformations.  Identifiability is a moment problem on the compact group `O(2)^s`.
3. **Two states (Theorem M).**  One hinge: never identifiable (Science division), and the
   competing ensembles may use reflections.  **Two hinges: identifiable among all ensembles,
   for all weights**, although `dim 𝒩(z) = 6` and (CC) fails: the chains that are linear
   images of the stacked configuration are exactly **eight**, the vertices of a cube
   generated by three reflections, with the two states at opposite vertices; their
   squared-distance matrices satisfy a single affine relation, the parity of the vertex,
   which no non-negative combination can use.  More hinges: identifiable, by overlapping
   triples of segments.
4. So for two states the dividing line is not the conic condition (four differing
   torsions) but **two differing torsions**; between two and three the certificate is not a
   rank of a linear system but a finite, explicit set of candidates.

## 1. Hinge families

A chain with fixed bond lengths and bond angles; `X` a conformation with `n` atoms; torsion
`m` is the dihedral of atoms `m, m+1, m+2, m+3` about the bond `(m+1, m+2)` (0-based, as in
`experiments/geometry/geomlib.py`).  Fix **hinges** `h_1 < ⋯ < h_s` (torsion indices).  The
**segments** are the atom sets

    S_0 = {0, …, h_1 + 2},   S_j = {h_j + 1, …, h_{j+1} + 2},   S_s = {h_s + 1, …, n − 1};

consecutive segments share the two atoms of the hinge bond, and every constrained pair
(bond or 1–3 pair) lies inside one segment.  Let `a_j` be the unit vector of the `j`-th hinge
bond of `X` and `O(2)_j ⊂ O(3)` the group of orthogonal maps that fix `a_j`: the rotations
about `a_j` and the reflections in planes containing `a_j`.  Put

    G = O(2)_1 × ⋯ × O(2)_s,      B_0 = I,   B_j(g) = g_1 g_2 ⋯ g_j .

For `g ∈ G` let `Y(g)` be the chain obtained from `X` by moving segment `j` by the isometry
with linear part `B_j(g)`, the translations being fixed by the shared hinge atoms.  Because
`B_{j−1}^{-1}B_j = g_j` fixes the hinge bond, consecutive segments agree on it: `Y(g)` is a
conformation with the bond lengths and angles of `X`.  The **hinge family** is
`𝒞_H(X) = {Y(g) : g ∈ G}` — `2^s` tori of dimension `s`; the conformations obtained from `X`
by changing the hinge torsions are the component of proper rotations.  If the segments
span space, different `g` give non-congruent chains.

**Lemma M1 (interval products) [P].**  For `i ∈ S_a`, `i' ∈ S_b`, `a ≤ b`, there are vectors
`u_a, …, u_b ∈ ℝ³` depending on `X, i, i'` only such that

    |Y(g)_{i'} − Y(g)_i|² = Σ_j |u_j|² + 2 Σ_{a ≤ j < l ≤ b} u_jᵀ (g_{j+1} g_{j+2} ⋯ g_l) u_l .

So every squared distance of `Y(g)` is an affine function of the **interval products**
`g_{[p,q]} = g_p ⋯ g_q`, `1 ≤ p ≤ q ≤ s`, and for an ensemble `ρ` on `G`

    M(ρ) = an affine function of the interval moments  m_{[p,q]}(ρ) = ∫ g_p ⋯ g_q dρ ∈ ℝ^{3×3}.

If every segment spans space, `M(ρ)` conversely determines all interval moments.

*Proof.*  With `c_j` the first atom of the `j`-th hinge bond in `X`,
`Y_{i'} − Y_i = B_a(c_{a+1} − x_i) + Σ_{a<j<b} B_j(c_{j+1} − c_j) + B_b(x_{i'} − c_b)`, a sum
`Σ B_j u_j`; expand the square and use `B_jᵀB_l = g_{j+1}⋯g_l`.  Conversely, for `b = a + 1` the
vectors `u_a`, `u_{a+1}` run over spanning sets as `i`, `i'` run over the two segments, so `M`
gives the bilinear form of `m_{[a+1,a+1]}`; for `b = a + 2` the only unknown term is
`u_aᵀ m_{[a+1,a+2]} u_{a+2}` with `u_a`, `u_{a+2}` again spanning; and so on by induction on
`b − a`.  ∎

*Relation to the Science division's statement (SCI-RES-0029 §3 (c)).*  For proper rotations
each `g_j` is affine in `(cos φ_j, sin φ_j)`, so `M` is a linear image of the Fourier
coefficients of the torsion distribution with indices in `{−1, 0, 1}^s` — their statement.
Lemma M1 says which image: only the nine entries of each interval product enter.  A product
over an interval of two or more hinges adds at most four numbers to those of its
sub-intervals (its first row in the direction `a_p` and its last column in the direction
`a_q` are given by them: `a_pᵀg_p = a_pᵀ`, `g_qa_q = a_q`), and a single rotation two.  So on
the torus at most `2s² + 1` combinations of the `3^s` coefficients enter — all nine for
`s = 2`, at most 19 of 27 for `s = 3` — and non-consecutive products such as `𝔼[g_1 g_3]` do
not enter at all.  And the group is `O(2)^s`, not the torus: with the reflections a single
hinge contributes four numbers.

## 2. Competing ensembles live on the hinge family

Call a segment **conic-free** if it spans space and its constrained edge vectors lie on no
conic (`𝒩 = 0` for the sub-chain; by Corollary 4.1 of the report this holds for generic
segments with at least five atoms and fails for four).

**Proposition M2 (reduction) [P].**  Let all segments be conic-free and let `μ` be an ensemble
on `𝒞_H(X)` — for instance a finite mixture of conformations that differ from `X` in the hinge
torsions only.  Then every ensemble `ν` on the full conformation space `𝒞` with
`M(ν) = M(μ)` is carried by `𝒞_H(X)`.  Consequently `μ` is identifiable from averaged squared
distances among all ensembles **iff** it is the only probability measure on `G` with its
interval moments.

*Proof.*  The atoms of a segment `S_j` form a chain on which every state of `μ` is congruent
to `X|_{S_j}`; so the averaged squared distances of `ν` between atoms of `S_j` are those of the
single conformation `X|_{S_j}`, which has `𝒩 = 0` and spans space.  By Theorem A of the report
(applied to the sub-chain) `ν`-almost every conformation restricted to `S_j` is congruent to
`X|_{S_j}`.  A conformation all of whose segments are congruent to those of `X` is `Y(g)` for
the `g` formed by the relative linear parts, which fix the shared hinge bonds.  The second
statement is Lemma M1.  ∎

The proposition does not use the stacked configuration, the number of states of `μ`, or
(CC).  It replaces the conformation space, whose dimension is `n − 3`, by a group of
dimension `s` — at the price of the `2^s` components.

## 3. Two states

Let `X_1 = X` and `X_2 = Y(r)`, `r = (R_1, …, R_s)` with `R_j` the rotation about `a_j` by
`δ_j`; `μ = w_1 δ_{X_1} + w_2 δ_{X_2}`, `w_1, w_2 > 0`.  As a measure on `G`:
`w_1 δ_e + w_2 δ_r`.

**Theorem M [P].**  Let all segments be conic-free.
(a) (*one hinge; the Science division's SCI-RES-0029 §3 (a), with reflections*)  For `s = 1`,
`μ` is never identifiable: the probability measures on `O(2)_1` with its data are exactly
those whose rotation part `ρ_+` and reflection part `ρ_−` satisfy
`∫ e^{iφ} dρ_+ = w_1 + w_2 e^{iδ_1}` and `∫ e^{2iψ} dρ_− = 0` (`ψ` the direction of the mirror
plane), a convex set of infinite dimension.
(b) (*two hinges*)  Let `s = 2`, `a_1 ∦ a_2`, and `δ_1, δ_2 ∉ πℤ`.  Let `a_{02}` be the axis of
`R_1R_2` and `ρ_A, ρ_B, σ` the reflections in the planes spanned by `(a_1, a_2)`,
`(a_1, a_{02})`, `(a_2, a_{02})`.  Then `R_1 = ρ_Bρ_A`, `R_2 = ρ_Aσ`, `R_1R_2 = ρ_Bσ`, and:
  (i) the conformations in `𝒞` that are linear images of the stacked configuration of `μ`
  are exactly the eight chains

      Y_{αβγ} = Y(ρ_B^β ρ_A^α, ρ_A^α σ^γ),   (α, β, γ) ∈ {0, 1}³,     X_1 = Y_{000},  X_2 = Y_{111}

  (segment 1 is moved by `ρ_B^βρ_A^α`, segment 2 by `ρ_B^βσ^γ`);
  (ii) their squared-distance matrices satisfy the affine relation
  `Σ (−1)^{α+β+γ} D(Y_{αβγ}) = 0`, and no other if `R_1R_2` is not a half-turn: they span an
  affine space of dimension 6;
  (iii) **`μ` is identifiable from averaged squared distances among all ensembles**, for all
  weights.  Yet `dim 𝒩(z) = 6`: the conic condition fails.
(c) (*more hinges*)  Let `s ≥ 2`, consecutive hinge axes non-parallel, all `δ_j ∉ πℤ`.  Then
`μ` is identifiable among all ensembles.

*Proof.*  By Proposition M2 we work with probability measures `ρ` on `G`.
(a) `m_{[1,1]}(ρ) = a_1a_1ᵀ ⊕ (∫ rot(φ) dρ_+ + ∫ refl(ψ) dρ_−)` on `ℝa_1 ⊕ a_1^⊥`, with
`rot(φ) = cos φ·1 + sin φ·J` and `refl(ψ) = cos 2ψ·K_1 + sin 2ψ·K_2` in a basis `1, J, K_1, K_2`
of the `2 × 2` matrices; the target `w_1·1 + w_2 rot(δ_1)` has no `K`-components.
(b) *Support.*  For `v = (v_0, v_1, v_2) ∈ (ℝ³)³`,
`∫ |v_0 + g_1v_1 + g_1g_2v_2|² dρ = Σ|v_j|² + 2Σ_{j<l} v_jᵀ m_{[j+1,l]} v_l` depends on the interval
moments only, so it equals `w_1|v_0 + v_1 + v_2|² + w_2|v_0 + R_1v_1 + R_1R_2v_2|²`, which
vanishes for `v = (a_{02}, 0, −a_{02})`.  Hence `g_1g_2a_{02} = a_{02}` `ρ`-almost surely.
(`R_1R_2 ≠ I` because the axes differ, and `a_{02}` is parallel to neither `a_1` nor `a_2`.)
Solve `g_2a_{02} = g_1^{-1}a_{02}` with `g_j ∈ O(2)_j`: since `g_2` fixes `a_2`, a solution `g_2`
exists iff `(g_1a_2)·a_{02} = a_2·a_{02}`, and then there are exactly two, one proper and one
improper, differing by `σ` on the right.  If `g_1` is the rotation about `a_1` by `θ`, the
condition reads `sin(θ/2)(q cos(θ/2) − p sin(θ/2)) = 0` with `(p, q) ≠ 0`: two solutions.  If
`g_1` is the reflection with unit normal `ν ⊥ a_1`, it reads `(ν·a_2)(ν·a_{02}) = 0`: `g_1 = ρ_A`
or `ρ_B`.  The pairs `(ρ_B^βρ_A^α, ρ_A^ασ^γ)` satisfy the support condition
(`g_1g_2 = ρ_B^βσ^γ` fixes `a_{02}`), they are eight, and `(ρ_Bρ_A, ρ_Aσ)` is a pair of proper
rotations different from `(I, I)`; the only such solution is `(R_1, R_2)`.  This proves the
three identities and that `ρ = Σ v_{αβγ} δ_{Y_{αβγ}}`.  The same computation in the language of
the report: `ρ`-almost every state is a linear image of the stack (Lemma 2.2), which is the
support condition; so (i).
*Weights.*  `m_{[1,1]}`: `Σ v_{αβγ} ρ_B^βρ_A^α = w_1 I + w_2 ρ_Bρ_A`.  On `a_1^⊥` the four matrices
`I, ρ_A, ρ_B, ρ_Bρ_A` are `1`, two reflections in lines at angle `δ_1/2`, and `rot(δ_1)`;
they are linearly independent iff `sin δ_1 ≠ 0`.  So `Σ_γ v_{10γ} = Σ_γ v_{01γ} = 0`, and by
non-negativity `v_{10γ} = v_{01γ} = 0`.  `m_{[2,2]}`: `Σ v_{αβγ} ρ_A^ασ^γ = w_1 I + w_2 ρ_Aσ`, and
in the same way `Σ_β v_{α β γ} = 0` for `α ≠ γ`: `v_{001} = v_{110} = 0`.  What remains is
`v_{000} = w_1`, `v_{111} = w_2`: (iii).
(ii) An affine relation `Σ c_{αβγ} D(Y_{αβγ}) = 0`, `Σ c = 0`, is by Lemma M1 a relation among
the triples `(g_1, g_2, g_1g_2) = (ρ_B^βρ_A^α, ρ_A^ασ^γ, ρ_B^βσ^γ)`; by the three independences
(the third: `I, σ, ρ_B, ρ_Bσ` on `a_{02}^⊥`) the sums of `c` along each axis of the cube vanish,
so `c` is a multiple of the parity, and the parity is a relation.  Eight points with one
relation span dimension 6.  `dim 𝒩(z) = 6`: §4.
(c) For `1 ≤ j < s` the sub-chain of the segments `S_{j−1}, S_j, S_{j+1}` is a two-hinge pair;
by (b) the restriction of `ρ` gives `(g_j, g_{j+1}) ∈ {(I, I), (R_j, R_{j+1})}` almost surely,
with probabilities `w_1, w_2`.  Since `R_j ≠ I`, the events `{g_j = I}` coincide for all `j`:
`ρ = w_1δ_e + w_2δ_r`.  ∎

**Remarks.**
1. *The cube.*  The eight candidates are the orbit of three commuting moves: reflect the
   middle segment in the plane parallel to both hinge bonds (`α`); reflect the last segment
   in the plane through the second hinge parallel to `a_{02}` (`γ`); reflect the middle and the
   last segment together in the plane through the first hinge parallel to `a_{02}` (`β`).
   Doing all three turns one state into the other: the composition of two rotations written
   with three reflections.  Six of the eight are "virtual" chains that no mixture can use.
2. *What decides.*  In (b) the support comes from one kernel vector of the moment matrix and
   the weights from two first moments; the mixed moment `m_{[1,2]}` is not needed for the
   weights.  The certificate of identifiability is the finite list of candidates together
   with the linear independence of four `2 × 2` matrices — not a rank of the conic system.
3. *Degenerate angles.*  For `δ_1 = π` the two mirror lines are perpendicular,
   `ρ_A + ρ_B = 2a_1a_1ᵀ` is no longer independent of `I` and `ρ_Bρ_A`, and the argument for the
   weights fails; we have not examined whether identifiability does.
4. *Threshold in atoms.*  Three conic-free segments need `h_1 ≥ 2`, `h_2 ≥ h_1 + 3`,
   `n ≥ h_2 + 6`: a two-hinge pair with identifiable mixture exists from `n = 11` atoms on
   (checked at `n = 11`), against `n = 10` for generic pairs (Theorem I) and never for one
   hinge.

## 4. The conic system of hinge pairs: the Science division's count is an equality

For two states with `s` hinges the stacked edge vectors of segment `j` lie in the
three-dimensional subspace `Δ_j = {(√w_1 v, √w_2 B_j(r)v)} ⊂ ℝ⁶`, and `Δ_i ∩ Δ_j` is the line
over the axis of `B_i^{-1}B_j`.  Since each segment is conic-free,
`𝒩(z) = {S ∈ Sym(6) : S vanishes on every Δ_j}`.  The Science division's bound is
`dim 𝒩(z) ≥ 21 − 6(s+1) + s(s+1)/2`, i.e. `10, 6, 3` for `s = 1, 2, 3`.

**Proposition M3 [P for `s ≤ 2`; N for `s = 3, 4, 5`].**  `dim 𝒩(z) = 10` for one hinge and
`= 6` for two hinges (under the hypotheses of Theorem M (b)).  Numerically it is `3` for
three hinges and `1` from four hinges on (segments of six atoms): (CC) holds exactly when
the two states differ in at least four torsions.

*Proof.*  `s = 2`: choose `e_1, e_2, e_3` on the lines `Δ_0 ∩ Δ_1`, `Δ_1 ∩ Δ_2`, `Δ_0 ∩ Δ_2` and
complete to bases `Δ_0 = ⟨e_1, e_3, f_0⟩`, `Δ_1 = ⟨e_1, e_2, f_1⟩`, `Δ_2 = ⟨e_2, e_3, f_2⟩`.  The sum
of the `Δ_j` is `ℝ⁶`: the second components `v_0 + R_1v_1 + R_1R_2v_2` with `v_0 + v_1 + v_2 = 0`
fill `ℝ³`, because the ranges of `R_1 − I` and `R_1R_2 − I` are the planes `a_1^⊥ ≠ a_{02}^⊥`.  So
these six vectors, which span the sum, are a basis.  `S` vanishes on the three subspaces iff the 6 diagonal entries and the 9 entries
`(e_1e_3), (e_1f_0), (e_3f_0), (e_1e_2), (e_1f_1), (e_2f_1), (e_2e_3), (e_2f_2), (e_3f_2)` vanish;
the entries `(e_1f_2), (e_2f_0), (e_3f_1), (f_0f_1), (f_0f_2), (f_1f_2)` are free.  `s = 1`: the
two subspaces span a hyperplane; in a basis `e_1, f_0, f_0', f_1, f_1', g` with `g` outside it,
11 entries vanish and 10 are free.  ∎

## 6. Several states at the same hinges; open ends

* **More than two states [N; exploration].**  For `K` states at `s` hinges the block moment
  matrix of the orientation tuples `(B_0, …, B_s)` has rank at most `3K`; its kernel always
  contains the `s` hinge relations `(…, a_j, −a_j, …)`, which every assembly satisfies, and has
  dimension `max(s, 3(s + 1) − 3K)`.  So the support of a competing ensemble is restricted
  only when `2s + 3 > 3K`; otherwise **every** assembly `Y(g)` is a linear image of the stack,
  and identifiability is purely a question about the interval moments.
  `experiments/geometry/hinge_multistate.py` (multi-start search for affine images of the
  stack over all chains, then a linear programme over the classes found):
  `K = 3, s = 2` — every start gives a new candidate, and in one of three instances an
  explicit ensemble of 13 chains reproduces every averaged squared distance to `10⁻¹³` with
  weight `0.62` on chains that are none of the states (and no weight on the third state):
  **three states at two hinges can fail to be identifiable**; in the two other instances the
  sampled programme found no competitor, which proves nothing.  `K = 3, s = 4` — only the
  three states are linear images (two further kernel relations): identifiable.
  `K = 3, s = 3`; `K = 4, s = 3, 4`; `K = 5, s = 4` — a continuum of candidates, no competitor
  found among 120 to 150 sampled ones: undecided.  (A count we first wrote down — finitely
  many candidates for `s ≥ ⌈3(K − 1)/2⌉` — was wrong: it treated the orthogonality of the
  `B_j` as independent conditions and is contradicted by `K = 3, s = 3`.)  On the torus of
  proper rotations and for two hinges this is the Science division's observation that three
  atoms leave one bilinear kernel polynomial, hence a curve of possible support.
* **Short segments.**  A segment with four atoms or fewer carries a conic and may deform in
  competing states; nothing here applies when two hinges are closer than three torsions.
* **A dual certificate.**  Identifiability in Theorem M (b) is proved by listing candidates.
  Whether the face `conv{D(X_1), D(X_2)}` of the realizable set is *exposed* — whether some
  `c_0 + ⟨C, D(·)⟩` is non-negative on all conformations and vanishes exactly on the two
  states — is open; such a function would give a stability statement of the kind of
  Theorem G′.
* **Other observables.**  If only block sums `⟨C_a, M⟩` are measured (low-angle scattering
  with labelled sets; SCI-RES-0029 §2), Proposition M2 fails as stated: the rigidity of each
  segment is no longer read off the data.  The stress certificate of the report has an
  immediate version for block sums (Theorem F″ below); no instance has been computed.

**Theorem F″ (stress certificate for linear functionals of `M`) [P].**  Let `μ` satisfy (CC)
with stacked configuration `z`, let the data be the numbers `⟨C_a, M⟩`, `a = 1, …, m`, for
symmetric matrices `C_a` with zero diagonal, and suppose the vectors
`(⟨C_a, D(X_k)⟩)_a`, `k = 1, …, K`, are affinely independent.  If there is `Ω ⪰ 0` with `Ω𝟙 = 0`,
`ker Ω = span(𝟙, columns of z)`, whose off-diagonal part outside the constrained pairs is a
linear combination of the `C_a`, then `μ` is identifiable from the data.
*Proof.*  As for Theorem F: `∫ tr(XᵀΩX) dν = −½⟨Ω, M(ν)⟩` is a function of the data and of the
statewise lengths, hence zero for every competing `ν`; the rest is unchanged.  ∎

## 7. Prior art

Searched on 2026-11-26 (three web searches, summaries only; log in `PRIOR_ART.md`, searches
9–11).  **Known:** the decomposition of a product of two rotations into three reflections
is classical (the usual proof that rotations compose to a rotation); mirror-image
ambiguities of fragments joined at an axis are familiar from distance geometry (the
discretizable molecular distance geometry problem branches by reflections through planes
of three consecutive atoms) **[K, from memory]**; truncated trigonometric moment problems on
tori, matrix-valued versions, and the flat-extension criterion for finitely atomic
representing measures (Curto–Fialkow and successors) are a developed theory **[K, from search
summaries]** — the support step of Theorem M (b) is its kernel argument, on a group with
several components and for moments of non-commuting products.  **Related, different
observable:** discrete "ghost" orientations of the domains of multi-domain proteins from
averaged orientational data (the fourfold degeneracy of relative orientations from one
alignment tensor, lifted by a second) **[K, from search summaries]** — a finite orbit of
spurious assemblies resolved by more data, like the six virtual chains here.  **Not found:**
the reduction of Proposition M2, the interval moments on `O(2)^s`, and Theorem M (the cube,
its single parity relation, identifiability from two hinges on).  An absence in three
summary-level searches is weak evidence; the Science division is asked to look.
The non-identifiability for one hinge and the failure of (CC) for at most three hinges are
the Science division's (SCI-RES-0029 §3).
