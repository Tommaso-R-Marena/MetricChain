# Results of Phase II, Workstream D: the pre-registered hypotheses, the comparisons, the supplementary experiments

*Computational Research Division, 2026-11-28.  The validation campaign was run by a subagent under
`PROTOCOL.md`; its code, raw rows and full tables are in `experiments/eng/certinf/` (`REPORT.md` there:
1 413 lines, generated by its `make_tables.py` from the stored rows; saved by the coordinator, because the
harness does not let a subagent write a report file).  This document is the coordinator's: the outcomes
strictly by the protocol's criteria, the numbers that carry the comparison, and what they do not show.
Every number below can be regenerated from stored files; where it is the coordinator's own aggregation
the script is named.*

## 0. In plain words

1. **Seven of the thirteen pre-registered hypotheses hold and six are refuted.**  Every refuted one is a
   prediction of mine about *how much* — an efficiency ratio, a growth rate, what the data add, what a
   wrong envelope does.  None concerns validity: **no certified bound of any of our methods was invalid
   in any of the 44 160 repetitions** in which one was computed (methods M1–M6).
2. **S3 as proposed is sound and, beyond an envelope ratio of about 3, practically empty**: it returns
   the whole range at a thousand draws for `Γ ≥ 10` and at every sample size tried for `Γ ≥ 30`.
3. **With S3's own inputs** (exact `b`, `G`, draws from the gap distribution) and the same confidence,
   replacing its inference rule by the one-function certificate shrinks the excess over the exact
   interval by a factor of about 7 to 50 at `Γ = 1.5` and 10 to 80 at `Γ = 3`, and gives intervals within
   2 % of exact at 10⁵ draws where S3 gives nothing (§3.1).
4. **From reference draws alone** the certified interval is 1.02 to 1.07 times as wide as the exact one
   at 10⁵ draws for every `Γ` up to 100, 1.1 to 1.5 times at 10⁴, and 1.9 to 3.6 times at 10³ (§3.2).
5. **Established practice.**  The bootstrap of plug-in bounds gives narrower intervals that miss an end
   of the identified interval in up to 16 % of repetitions at a nominal 5 %.  Maximum-entropy reweighting
   gives one number, not an interval.  A model-free programme on the sampled pool is 1.2 to 51 times as
   wide as the identified interval and is not a statement about unsampled configurations.  The
   information-theoretic bound is wider than the exact bound without data in every cell (§3.4).
6. **Negative: the measured averages add little here.**  The exact interval without data is only 1.35
   times as wide as the exact interval with data (median over the 27 cells of F1–F3), and less than 1.5
   times in 10 of the 22 cells with `Γ ≥ 3`.
7. **Negative: my cost prediction was wrong.**  On the test family the number of reference draws needed
   for a fixed accuracy grows like `Γ^0.37`, not like `Γ` (§2, H-D).  The linear law is the worst case.
8. **Inconclusive: a wrong envelope.**  With the true energy error twice the declared one, the certified
   interval never missed the true value; the test was too weak to produce a miss (§3.6).
9. **Supplementary, not pre-registered:** draws from the extremal ensemble (Theorem 5.4) remove the
   dependence on `Γ` — an excess of 0.01 to 0.02 at `Γ = 100` where reference draws give 0.15 to 0.18 —
   and cost a little at small `Γ` (§4.1).  The outer bound for references that are not products is sound in 24 of 24
   cases and up to 2.6 times too wide (§4.2).

### 3.4 Established approaches

* **B1, percentile bootstrap of plug-in sharp bounds** (`n = 10³` only).  Median width 1.13 times the
  exact width — narrower than any certificate at that sample size — but it misses an end of the
  identified interval in 3 % to 16 % of repetitions per cell at `Γ ≥ 10` (8 of 10 cells above the nominal
  5 %), and in 8.4 % over all its 1 800 repetitions.  It always contained the true value.  It is a good
  estimate and not a certificate.
* **B2, maximum-entropy reweighting.**  One number.  It lies in the identified interval in 99.9 % of
  repetitions and within 0.002 of the true value in the median — because the true ensemble of these
  instances is itself an exponential tilt of the reference.  It carries no interval; its effective
  sample size falls from 99.8 % to 83 % of `n` between `Γ = 1.5` and `100`.
* **B3, model-free programme on the sampled pool.**  1.2 to 51 times as wide as the identified interval
  where it was run (the ratio falls as the envelope widens, because the identified interval grows), and a
  statement about the pool only.
* **B4, the information bound** is 1.15 to 2.9 times as wide as **B5**, the exact threshold rule without
  data, in every cell.  B5 is 1.02 to 2.33 times as wide as the exact interval with data (median 1.35).

### 3.6 Sensitivity

**(a) The envelope is wrong** (true half-width `2ρ`, declared `ρ`; nine cells, 50 repetitions).  The
certified interval contained the true value in every repetition.  In seven cells the true value lies
inside the exact interval of the declared class anyway.  In the other two (F2, `Γ = 3` and `10`) no
ensemble of the declared class is compatible with the data, and the inconsistency certificate says so in
68 % and 94 % of repetitions.  **So this test did not show that a wrong envelope is harmless; it showed
that these instances do not punish one.**  A wrong envelope remains undetectable in general.

**(b) What the observations add** (F1, `Γ = 10`): the exact width is 0.614 with no observation or an
uninformative one, 0.584 with one, 0.511 with both.

**(c) Inconsistent data** (36 cells, `Γ ≤ 3`, `n = 10⁴`): no false alarm in 300 repetitions with consistent
data; detection in every repetition at a displacement of 0.1 or more; at 0.05, in every repetition on F1
and F3 and in 68 % and 42 % on F2.

### 3.7 Structure

* **F4**: exact interval of a `2¹⁰⁰`-state product by level counts in 6 to 8 s; two solvers agree.
* **F5**: the site-wise formula of Corollary 9.3 equals the programme over the whole cube in 16 cases to
  `2.4·10⁻¹³`, in under 0.15 s where the programme takes 12 to 75 s at 12 sites; 200 sites take 2.7 s and
  the intervals stay informative (for instance `[−0.50, −0.05]`).  The box hull of the same class is
  within 0.085 of the whole range at 8 sites, `1.6·10⁻⁴` at 20, `4·10⁻¹¹` at 50.
* **F6**: the ensemble division's three frozen model-free controls are reproduced to `10⁻¹⁶`.

### 4.3 The sequential bound (before the review)

## 5. What is shown, after every allowance

* On enumerable models with envelopes that hold by construction and exact samplers, a **valid** interval
  for an ensemble average under an unknown energy within an envelope, with measured averages, is obtained
  from 10⁴ to 10⁵ reference draws to within a few per cent of the exact interval for envelope ratios up
  to 100 — where S3 returns the whole range, where the bootstrap is narrower and invalid, and where
  reweighting returns a number without an interval.
* Inconsistency between data and class is certified without false alarms.
* For bounded-influence classes with product references and additive observables the interval is exact
  at 200 sites in seconds, where every rectangular class is vacuous.

## 6. Where the methods lose or add nothing

* **At 10³ draws** every certificate is 2 to 4 times too wide, and empty from `Γ = 30`; the bootstrap is
  the better *estimate* there.
* **The data added little** on these instances (median factor 1.35); a user who only wants the interval
  without data needs no sampling at all (the threshold rule is exact).
* **S3's inputs help only for `Γ ≤ 3`**, and need a pass over the whole space.
* **The extremal-ensemble certificate is worse than the reference one for `Γ ≤ 3`**, needs a sampler that
  may not exist, and falls back to the reference rate under rejection sampling.
* **F2 at `Γ = 100`**: excess 0.12 even at 10⁵ reference draws.
* **Non-product references**: the only bound available is up to 2.6 times too wide at modest couplings.
* **Six predictions of mine were wrong** (§2), and one baseline was biased in my favour until the review
  found the defect (§1).

## 7. Limits of this evidence

Synthetic models; envelopes true by construction; the true ensemble an exponential tilt correlated with
the observables; exact samplers; exact references in floating point; repetitions cut to 100 and 30; the
bootstrap at one sample size; one sweep without error bars for H-D; supplementary experiments not
pre-registered and designed after seeing the review.  Nothing here is evidence about a molecule, and
nothing was run on held-out material.
