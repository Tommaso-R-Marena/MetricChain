## C. Negative results that constrain software design

**C1. D42 / Thm 11.37 / Thm 11.36: per-torsion constants cannot certify long chains.** The verified constants satisfy `c_i ≥ 0.824·Λ b m_i^{3/2}` (`m_i` = distance to the nearer chain end); no valid constants lie below about `0.186·Λ b m_i^{3/2}` [N], a figure GEOMETRY raised to about 0.6 [N]. With `c_i ≥ c₀ m_i^γ`, `γ > 0`: `ln N_cov ≥ n ln k − C((G+ε)/c₀)^{1/(1+γ)}`, so only `O(N^{1/3})` end sites are sharp. Every randomised algorithm pays `(1−2δ)` times this, and the same holds for `ln Z` at extensive accuracy. **Scope:** zero residual, additive known part, product rotamer domain, excluded volume ignored, fixed `L`, weighted-Hamming format. AA1 S6/E46 extends this: on every torsion domain the arm inertia is `≥ 0.0267·m b²`, so the constants are `≥ 0.33 Λ b m^{1/2}` and "excluded volume does not rescue the route". For contact-type residuals every torsion-Hamming envelope has `c_i ≥ u₀⌊(m_i−1)/2⌋`. Design consequence (TR l.4141–4148): constants stay bounded only for residuals local along the sequence or in windowed local frames. In practice: certify side chains on a **fixed backbone**, not torsion-space backbones.

**C2. D28 (Thms 11.8–11.9, 11.13, §11.10): the exponential price of first-order classes, for every algorithm.** One evaluation certifies iff every site is sharp; otherwise the count is exponential in the non-sharp sites (`(6/5)ⁿ` even at the zero residual, `binary_certificate_exponential` [V]). For `ln Z`, at least `exp(entropy)` evaluations are needed even for sharp landscapes (`card_ge_entropy` [V]). The bounds are *instance-wise*: "a cheaper paradigm necessarily gives up correctness on the first-order class and should say which members it gives up" (D28 converse reading). "Assume benign, fall back if contradicted" is unavailable. Design consequence: choose the *class* (second-order with small constants, linear-in-features, box with a small floor distance), not a cleverer search.

**C3. Dense second-order classes (Thms 11.31–11.32; D32, D40).** The remainder is quadratic in Hamming distance. For any evaluation set the uncertainty is `≥ c·C(d+1,2)` at distance `d` (`dip_witness` [V]); stars do not combine (`stars_do_not_combine` [V]); the cost is `2^{n−o(n)}` unless `e ≳ c·n/4`. AUD §1a: at a residual with second differences up to `κc` the constants become `(1∓κ)c`, and at the class boundary the statement fails.

**C4. "Measured constants are not certified ones"** (README l.179–180; TR l.2336–2343, l.2462). Every constant `c_ij`, `c_T`, floor and variance quoted for a real energy term "was obtained by enumeration and is a price, not a certificate" (TR l.1286–1288). AUD §4 item 6: slopes, floors, variances and per-pair constants "are inputs that nothing in the repository certifies for a concrete residual". The one exception is SDD's paper certificate (double precision with margins, evaluation tolerance assumed, one implementation).

**C5. Sampling statistics are not certificates (D56 item 3, DEAD.28; §11.30).** Mean + 2 SE of Boltzmann factors is false at every sample size for a rare deep dip (frequency 0.37). The number of samples below which nothing is certified is set by the a-priori floor `e^{ρ₋}/ε`. SDD found sup-norm remainders estimated on samples exceeded on held-out conformations by up to ×10. Polynomial moments (mean, RMS fit error) certify nothing; exponential moments (Rényi) do (Prop. 11.112; D57 item 6).

**C6. Box-class cost is extensive (D57 item 5; TR l.1274–1288).** The free-energy distance to the floor was 0.07–243 kT on 74 instances, linear in chain length; "the exponential is renamed, not removed". Exact sampling of the known part is needed (product or small treewidth only).

**C7. Sign-blind bounds lose intrinsically (§12 item 13(a)).** A bound using only charge magnitudes is 16–153× the truth at `m = 3–5` (leucine helix) and unbounded for neutral clusters. For net-charged clusters the loss is `≤ (1+ρ)/(1−ρR²)·R²`, but SDD's real blocks have `ρR² ≥ 4.67`, outside the lemma's regime. Polar and charged side chains remain open.

**C8. Ball necessity (Prop. 11.18e(a), [P]).** On a class closed under adding lower-order functions, every deterministic certificate evaluates at least `N_ball` configurations on every input. The randomised analogue was **false as written** (D60): `⌈k^{m−1}ln(1/δ)⌉` random evaluations give a valid but useless bound. At prescribed width it is [C].

**C9. Non-product (clash-pruned) domains (Prop. 11.18c; D59 items 3–4).** Bounds hold on the reset-closed part only, "connectivity is not enough". Completeness of shares fails (two-site counterexample). SDD's pruned products were smaller than the reset-closed part in 34 of 40 analyses, and the clash-free minimum was outside it in 14. Also D1/L1 C5: exact dominance on pruned domains is coNP-hard.

**C10. Partition kernel limits (D22, D33).** Leaf/branch/prune alone need `2ⁿ` leaves on a chain. Separations hold only for exact lower ends and depend on the representation (gauge-trivial instance needs 4 leaves). Lower bounds for decomposition-aware or approximate certificates are open.

**C11. Search and certificate hardness (Thm 11.10/G5; D26, D30, D39; TR §8 l.1183–1189).** Minimal certificates are NP-hard to find; no search is instance-optimal; checking anchor certificates on succinct product spaces is coNP-hard (covering radius). The kernels avoid this only through explicit branch-and-bound certificates.

**C12. Soundness hygiene (D15, D16).** Unguarded kernels were vacuous on inconsistent data and accepted absurd bounds. Only the `…Top` entry points are safe. Invariance is for a parameter group, SE(3) is not defined, and distance-based certificates are chirality-blind (§7 item 3).

**C13. Geometry bounds are worst-case ℓ¹ (D9, D10).** Coaxial ≠ Kabsch; random torsion errors are overestimated by about √m.

**C14. Dependent references (D14, D25, D41).** The `Σc²` width fails for every correlation `ρ > 0` (AA1 S2). Worst residuals are not additive for Markov trees or pairwise models, and the evidence failed because violating sets are thin (`<10⁻⁴` relative volume).

**C15. Engineering facts.** Lean-kernel evaluation of `verify` does not scale (11.9 GB at 512 entries). `native_decide` is forbidden, so the PBLean route adds a trusted component (L1 C4(ii) addendum). numpy `Generator.binomial` is biased for `n ≳ 10¹⁵`, `np ≥ 44` (D57 item 4): relevant to any sampler software.

## D. Guarantees the division itself calls computationally vacuous

From AUD §5 (l.393–408) unless noted:
1. Thm 11.33(a), polynomial for `w ≤ 4`: about `n⁵ε⁻²`, above `2ⁿ` up to n≈22–40.
2. Thm 11.33(b): asymptotic; `e^{3.9}` at n=2000, w=5.
3. Any-reference sandwich width `(Σc_i)²`: vacuous once `βΣc_i > 8`.
4. Stratified-sample optimality "up to a polynomial factor" (11.76(a), 11.95): the factor exceeds the cost for n≤50 / n≤300, and is empty with one type per site.
5. Asymptotic isoperimetry (Levels 1–2): useful only from `n≈10¹¹–5·10¹⁴`.
6. Profile-certificate cost bounds (Prop. 11.49): vacuous at extensive accuracy (`10^{93}`–`10^{2697}`; D43 item 5).
7. Voxel enclosures in dimension `d`: `d^{d/2}` (AA1 E8: vacuous beyond a handful of coupled continuous coordinates).
8. Abstract nesting theorem with a non-sharp tail function: exponent 0.197 instead of 0.029; sample cap `10^{14}`–`10^{24}`.
9. Nested adaptive algorithm at bounded accuracy (11.56, 11.58, 11.58a): the schedule costs more than `n·a` up to n≈2·10⁴.
10. Rate theorem for `k` levels: no certified constants at finite `k`.
Further, outside AUD §5:
11. Per-torsion envelope constants as input to certification: "vacuous for long chains" (AA1 E12).
12. Soundness theorems on unchecked data: vacuously true (D15; AA1 E14).
13. The transfer-matrix (Boltzmann) bound for chains as known parts: "vanishes on the window `2h < c ≤ 2(J+h)`" (row G17).
14. First version of Thm 11.44: certified nothing below accuracy about 6.7 (D43 item 1, repaired).

## F. Open questions / proof obligations that real-structure software will hit

1. **Certified envelope / mixed-difference / order-`m` constants for one real energy term**, obtained without enumeration. "Without which the interface is hypothetical" (§12 items 4, 7, 9(f); TR §8 l.1159–1161, l.1190–1193; Thm 11.18a l.2342).
2. **Interval evaluation of force-field terms over voxels** (table enclosures are inputs; TR §8 l.1151–1155, §6.4 l.1076–1078; AA1 E8). This includes a certified positive lower bound on `r²` for Lennard-Jones poles (AA1 E13).
3. **Producer and scalable checker:** a refinement proof that the integer leaf checker implies `LeafOK`, an integer checker for whole trees, and a compiled checker (§12 item 7; TR l.2506–2516).
4. **A measured comparison of native certificates against PB proof logging** (VeriPB/PBLean) for the same run, and the trust-base vs scale trade-off (§6.4 l.1057–1064; SCI-REV-0006 §7).
5. **A certified floor in compact geometries;** polar and charged side chains; compact ensembles with real side-chain entropy; whether a sign-aware bound can be uniform over contexts (§12 item 13(a)); the nested sampler on a real sandwich (13(g)).
6. **Exact sampling** of the known part. Box classes need it; only product and small-treewidth known parts qualify. A known part sampled by a Markov chain with certified mixing is open (§12 13(d)).
7. **Non-product domains** (sterically excluded pairs): restriction to the reset-closed part, and coNP-hard dominance (Prop. 11.18c; L1 C5). Excluded volume is ignored in the chain no-go and its count (AA1 E47 `{?}`).
8. **Integrality gaps:** when shares fail, branch. One branch equals the triplet constraints through the site (SDD P7.3, unverified by Theory). It is open whether a connected family needs exponentially many `sum`-free leaves (§12 item 6).
9. **Query complexity of certifying a ratio of macrostate weights** with geometry (§12 item 11(a)). Also open: the exact range for a pairwise known part on trees (11(b)), and counting-statistic macrostates (for example the number of contacts).
10. **Noisy / floating-point evaluations:** "bounded errors give accuracy floors by a one-line argument; variance is not treated anywhere" (AUD §8 item 8). SDD's certificate assumes the evaluation tolerance.
11. **Probabilistic-certificate kernel:** a checker of spot-check transcripts with verifier-side randomness, and certification of the detection probability (§12 item 8). No sampling certificate is checked by any kernel (TR l.1295–1297).
12. **The complexity of the exact criteria:** is "rotamer `r` possibly optimal" `Σ₂ᵖ`-hard, and polynomial on chains? (§12 item 3).
13. **Continuous geometry:** flexible bond geometry (AA1 E46 `{?}`), SE(3) not defined in Lean, Eckart-frame sharpening of the ℓ¹ chain rule (TR l.873–875), a non-product base measure or Jacobian for voxels (AA1 E8).
14. **Unrefereed parts a product would depend on:** Thm 11.18a, Props 11.18b(b)–(d) and 11.18c, the duality of 11.18b(e), Prop. 11.50 (linear kernel), §11.30 except the reviewed parts, §11.31 barriers, and Prop. 11.113a (status/theory.md l.156–180; RESEARCH_LOG "Resume here" l.2800–2810).
15. **Program context:** a consolidation directive is expected, and the computational hard stop is 2026-11-30 15:00 ET (RESEARCH_LOG l.2775–2778). The theory division's current priority ("Theorem C", cube exponents) has no bearing on structural-biology software (my inference).

**Bottom line (my inference).** The translatable core is the soundness layer: the min, residual and partition kernels, the voxel bridge, the second/third-order share certificates and the anchored-expansion identity. It is sound and machine-checked, but everything that would make it *bite* on a real protein is missing: certified constants, interval force-field tables, a producer and a scalable checker. The complexity theory is mainly useful negatively. It tells STRUCTBIO to stay on fixed backbones, to avoid first-order per-torsion envelopes, and to expect certification to fail where the side-chain ensemble is not frozen.
