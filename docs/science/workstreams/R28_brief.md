## Item 1 — Proposition A17.24 (item 97), line by line

Re-derive every part: the cell-wise identity; the lower end (Jensen with cell means); the upper end with its
two modes per (site, cell) — bounded differences with the moment-generating-function bound, and the cell
minimum; the claim "second order in the cell size".  Questions to answer: Is the reference measure inside a
cell required to be a product, and is that stated?  Is the bounded-difference constant the right one when a
term depends on several sites?  Are closed cells with a trapezoid measure (end points shared by two cells with
half weights) a legitimate decomposition, or a grid artefact that changes the statement?  Does the "tilted
reference" of part (26) (one-site slices through the grid minimum) satisfy the hypotheses, and is the
remainder what the text says it is?  What exactly is proved about a *continuum*, as opposed to a grid model —
is any sentence stronger than that?  For each hypothesis: essential / needed only by the proof / convenience /
replaceable by a weaker quantitative condition / removal changes the constants / removal makes it false —
with an example where you claim "false".  If you find a counterexample to any stated inequality, give the
smallest one.
