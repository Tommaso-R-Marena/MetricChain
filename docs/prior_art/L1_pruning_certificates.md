# L1 prior-art check: dominance pruning under gauges and residuals, thermal dominance, certificate complexity

Literature check performed on 2026-11-24 for the MetricChain program (discrete sites `i = 1..L` with finite state sets plus continuous torsions; `E = E0 + sum_i h_i + sum_ij J_ij + sum_ijk T_ijk + Delta_res`, where `Delta_res` is a non-decomposable black-box residual known only through bounds). Covers candidate claims C1-C5 and a summary of the contemporary provable baselines.

## Reading guide

- **Verdicts.** `KNOWN` (a citation states it), `KNOWN-IN-SPIRIT / FOLKLORE` (an immediate corollary or routine combination of cited results, or stated in a neighbouring field), `NO MATCH FOUND` (the listed searches did not surface it; this is not a proof of novelty).
- **Evidence tags.** `[V]` = bibliographic record confirmed online in this session **and** the quoted statement, equation or theorem was read in the paper's own text (publisher, arXiv, PubMed Central or author copy). `[V-abs]` = record confirmed, but only the abstract or metadata was readable, so theorem numbers and body content are not checked. `[unverified-from-memory]` = recalled, not confirmed online in this session; treat as a lead, not as a citation.
- **Sources of verification.** Biomedical records and several full texts come from PubMed / PubMed Central (NCBI); other records from Crossref, arXiv, DROPS and publisher pages. DOIs are given so every item can be re-checked.
- **How texts were read.** PDFs (arXiv, author copies) and PubMed Central full texts were text-extracted and the quoted passages read directly. HTML pages (software documentation, repository READMEs, competition pages, some abstracts) were read through an automated fetch-and-summarise tool; those entries say so or are limited to short quoted phrases.
- **Conference names.** For book chapters in proceedings series, the DOI, title, authors, pages and year were confirmed in Crossref; the conference acronym attached to them (for example CADE, IJCAR, CPAIOR, NFM, VSTTE, TYPES, CICM) is from memory unless the entry quotes a proceedings volume.
- **Reviewer's derivations.** A few short arguments are mine (marked as such). They show why a claim is or is not a corollary of the cited literature. They have not been refereed. The ones for C1, C2, C3, C4(i) and C5 were sanity-checked by brute force on small random instances with throwaway scripts that are not part of the repository.
- **Baseline.** Each claim is compared against the strongest contemporary work found (2015-2026), not only the 1990s papers named in the brief.
- **Fast-moving items.** Lean 4 and proof-logging projects (LeanVIPR, PBLean, LeanCSP, VeriPB kernel format) change monthly. Details reflect what was fetched on 2026-11-24.
- **Limits of this check** are listed in section 8. The most important: the full texts of Lecoutre et al. 2012 and Goldstein 1994 were not accessible, the web-search budget ran out before two planned sweeps, and the equations of Gainza et al. 2012 are images in the online version.

## C1. Gauge invariance of dead-end elimination

**Claim as checked.** For a pairwise model, (a) Goldstein's criterion `G_i(r,t) = h_i(r) - h_i(t) + sum_j min_s [J_ij(r,s) - J_ij(t,s)]` is unchanged by every reparameterisation `h_i += sum_j m_{j->i}`, `J_ij -= m_{j->i} + m_{i->j}`; (b) Desmet's criterion `D_i(r,t) = h_i(r) - h_i(t) + sum_j [min_s J_ij(r,s) - max_s J_ij(t,s)]`, maximised over all reparameterisations, equals `G_i(r,t)`; (c) so optimising the LP-dual gauge cannot improve Goldstein pruning.

**Reviewer's derivation (the claim is correct and takes three lines).** Messages into site `i` cancel between the unary and pairwise parts of both criteria. Goldstein's criterion sees `J_ij` only through `d_j(s) = J_ij(r,s) - J_ij(t,s)`, which `m_{i->j}(s)` does not change, so (a) holds. For (b), per neighbour, `min_s [J(r,s) - m(s)] - max_s [J(t,s) - m(s)] <= d_j(s*)` for every `s*`, hence `<= min_s d_j(s)`, with equality at `m_{i->j}(s) = J_ij(t,s)`. The only question is therefore whether the literature already contains it.

**Verdict.** KNOWN-IN-SPIRIT / FOLKLORE. **Confidence:** high that the statement carries no publishable novelty by itself; medium that the literal Desmet sentence is nowhere in print (the bodies of Lecoutre et al. 2012, Goldstein 1994 and the 2014 journal paper were not read).

**Three caveats the claim should carry.**
- It holds for finite costs on a full product domain. With an incumbent upper bound (bounded addition) or pruned tuples, the practical tests do depend on the gauge (de Givry et al., Eq. 4).
- "Cannot improve pruning" is true per test at fixed domains. Interleaving with gauge-dependent value removal changes the domains and therefore changes what DEE prunes next.
- Gauge-optimised multi-site substitutions subsume Goldstein's DEE (Shekhovtsov 2014, quoted above). The claim must not be read as "the gauge does not help dominance-type pruning".

**How to sharpen it into something new.**
1. Prove a classification rather than a single invariance. Tests that are functions of the neighbourhood difference energy (Goldstein singles and pairs, conformational splitting, "never a best response") are gauge-invariant. Tests built from bounds (Desmet, A* and `f_∅` pruning, incumbent-aware DEE) are not. For each of the latter, compute the optimal gauge in closed form, as is done above for Desmet. The open part is the incumbent-aware regime, where the WCSP literature says exact dominance becomes intractable: what is the strongest polynomial incumbent-aware single-site rule, and is its optimal gauge the VAC/OSAC dual?
2. Move to 3-body terms, where exact dominance is coNP-hard (C5). Compare three computable relaxations: the nested-min rule used in many-body DEE (LuCore et al. 2015, C2 item 7), the term-wise criterion optimised over gauges, and the local-polytope LP on the neighbour difference model. Shekhovtsov's Theorem 3.3 says the last two coincide. A strict hierarchy with examples, plus exactness when the link graph of the site is a forest, would be new for the protein-design literature.

## C2. Dominance pruning with a non-decomposable residual

**Claim as checked.** With `E = E_dec + Delta_res`: (a) if only `lo <= Delta_res <= hi` is known, prune `r` against `t` when the Goldstein margin of `E_dec` exceeds `hi - lo`; (b) if a differential envelope `delta_i(r,t) <= Delta_res(r, x_-i) - Delta_res(t, x_-i)` is known, prune when the margin exceeds `-delta_i(r,t)`; each criterion is information-theoretically tight for its class.

**Closest prior art.**

1. I. Georgiev, R. H. Lilien, B. R. Donald, "The minimized dead-end elimination criterion and its application to protein redesign in a hybrid scoring and search algorithm for computing partition functions over molecular ensembles", J. Comput. Chem. 29(10):1527-1542 (2008), [doi:10.1002/jcc.20909](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcc.20909), PMC3263346. `[V]`
   - MinDEE criterion (their Eq. 2), with `E_min`, `E_max` and `E_range = E_max - E_min` the minimum, maximum and range of an energy term over its voxel: `E_min(i_r) + sum_j min_s E_min(i_r,j_s) - sum_j max_s E_range(j_s) - sum_{j<k} max_{s,u} E_range(j_s,k_u) > E_max(i_t) + sum_j max_s E_max(i_t,j_s)`.
   - The motivation is the non-decomposability introduced by continuous minimisation: "changing rotamer `i_r` to `i_t` does not affect the energy term `E(j_s)`; however, when energy minimization is allowed, the value of this energy term may change ... Therefore, to be provably correct, one must account for a range of possible energies."
   - Reviewer's reading: the range terms bound how much the rest of the system can change when site `i` is swapped. That is a bounded-difference envelope in the sense of C2(b), with `delta_i = -(sum of ranges)`, in a Desmet-type form.
   - Their Eq. 3 adds an energy window `E_w` to the right-hand side so that no conformation within `E_w` of the optimum is pruned.
2. P. Gainza, K. E. Roberts, B. R. Donald, "Protein design using continuous rotamers", PLoS Comput. Biol. 8(1):e1002335 (2012), [doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002335](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002335). `[V]` for the prose; the equations are images online, so the formula below is reconstructed and tagged `[unverified-from-memory]`.
   - iMinDEE prunes with Goldstein's form on the lower-bound (pairwise-minimised) energies and a pruning interval `I`: `E_min(i_r) - E_min(i_t) + sum_j min_s [E_min(i_r,j_s) - E_min(i_t,j_s)] > I`.
   - Prose confirmed: iMinDEE removes "the maximum energy bounds" from the MinDEE criterion; `I` is "the difference between" the best minimised energy found and the lowest energy bound, and "can potentially be reduced either by finding a conformation with a lower energy, or by improving the lower bound"; the procedure "must only be repeated at most once" and "is guaranteed to find the minGMEC".
   - Reviewer's derivation of what this means for C2(a): write `LB = E_dec + lo`. For any evaluated conformation `x^`, `I = E(x^) - min LB` is a sound margin, because `LB(r,x) > LB(t,x) + I >= min LB + I = E(x^) >= E*`. Taking `x^` as the minimiser of `E_dec` gives `I <= hi - lo`, with equality only if the residual attains `hi` there. One evaluation of the residual therefore gives a margin that is never larger than `hi - lo` and is usually smaller.

**Verdict.** (a) KNOWN-IN-SPIRIT. (b) special case KNOWN (MinDEE); general formulation and tightness NO MATCH FOUND. **Confidence:** medium-high for (a), medium for (b).

**Problems with the tightness claim as stated (reviewer's analysis).**
- It is a statement about pointwise dominance, not about safe pruning. Under class (a), `r` can be optimal for some admissible residual iff `min_x E_dec(r,x) - min E_dec <= hi - lo`. The exact safe-pruning test is this window condition. The Goldstein-margin test is a polynomial relaxation of it, tight only in the minimax sense over instances.
- It holds only for criteria that never evaluate the residual. A black box can usually be queried, and iMinDEE shows that a single query reduces the required margin from `hi - lo` to `E(x^) - min LB`.
- For class (b) the envelope must be closed under composition. Since `Delta(r) - Delta(u) = [Delta(r) - Delta(v)] + [Delta(v) - Delta(u)]`, the implied bound is the longest-path closure `delta*_i(r,u) = max over paths`. The criterion with a non-closed `delta_i` is not tight. Tightness is a statement about difference constraints on the Hamming graph (existence of a feasible potential in which a given edge is tight).

**How to sharpen it into something new.**
1. State and prove the query-model version. With `q` evaluations of the residual, the optimal margin is `min over evaluated x^ of [E(x^) - min LB]`, with a matching adversarial lower bound. This would explain iMinDEE's pruning interval as optimal in a precise sense and extend it to two-sided `[lo, hi]` and to envelopes. No such optimality statement was found.
2. Make the envelope the object of study: closure, composition across sites, and certified construction for three concrete residuals. These are a neural correction (certified bound propagation), the truncated many-body remainder of LuCore et al., and fixed-point rounding of energies in cost-function-network encodings. In the last case a differential envelope scales with the degree of site `i` while the global range scales with the number of terms, which makes (b) strictly more useful than (a).

## C3. Thermal dominance

**Claim as checked.** If `E(r, x_-i) >= E(t, x_-i) + gamma` for all `x_-i`, then `Z_{i=r} <= exp(-beta*gamma) Z_{i=t}`; pruning dominated states changes `ln Z` by at most `ln(1 + n exp(-beta*gamma))`; the statement generalises to any measure-preserving injective map `T` with `E(Tx) <= E(x) - gamma`.

**Reviewer's derivation of where it sits.** The exact identity is `Z_{i=r} / Z_{i=t} = < exp(-beta [E(r,x) - E(t,x)]) >`, the average being over `x_-i` drawn from the Boltzmann distribution conditioned on `i = t`. This is the free-energy-perturbation identity. The claim is its worst-case bound. For a pairwise model the exponent is `a + sum_j d_j(x_j)`, so the worst case is Goldstein's criterion read at finite temperature. The map version is the injection argument used in Peierls-type proofs.

**Verdict.** The inequality is FOLKLORE. Its use as a certified pruning rule for partition functions is NO MATCH FOUND. **Confidence:** medium. The bodies of the BBK* and MARK* papers were not readable in full, and the injection argument is standard enough that an unindexed remark may exist.

**Caveats.**
- The bound needs `gamma` of order `RT ln(n / epsilon)` per site, and errors add over pruning rounds as `sum ln(1 + m_k exp(-beta gamma_k))`, where `m_k` is the largest number of pruned states mapped to one kept state.
- K* scores are ratios of partition functions, so each state needs a two-sided statement.
- With continuous torsions the sums become integrals over basins. Pointwise dominance then needs a measure-preserving map between basins, or dominance must be stated for basin free energies. Basins of different volume introduce a Jacobian or volume factor that the discrete statement hides. The sibling report `L2_relaxations_partition.md` (item C8(b)) covers what K*-type algorithms do and do not bound over a voxel.

**How to sharpen it into something new.**
1. Present it as a certificate, and compare with the `p*` bound. The witness is the list of pruned states with a dominator and a margin each; checking is one Goldstein evaluation per witness. Unlike `k exp(-E_0/RT)`, the bound does not grow with the number of pruned conformations. An explicit instance family where `p*` forces re-introduction of pruned rotamers while the dominance bound is already below `epsilon` would make the comparison concrete.
2. Go beyond the worst case. Because the exact ratio is an expectation under the conditional distribution, any certified bound on neighbour marginals (for example from weighted mini-bucket or Hölder bounds) gives a strictly better certified ratio than the minimum. A "thermal Goldstein criterion with partial marginal information" was not found.

**Reviewer's remarks.**
- The transfer to MAP is direct. A leaf that contains an assignment optimal in every component can only be closed if its bound equals the optimum in every component, because a component's bound never exceeds its optimum on such a leaf. For an antiferromagnetic triangle this forces at least one fixed variable per component. Two complementary optima of a triangle cannot share such a leaf, so the product of these fooling pairs gives `2^n` leaves.
- The same argument gives a general lower bound: the product over components of the fooling number of its optimal set with respect to bound-closed boxes. A component whose optima all lie in one closed box contributes no factor. For such components the tree size is governed by how the excess of suboptimal branches pays for the gaps of components not yet branched, which is a knapsack-like accounting.
- No special rule is needed for the `O(n)` side. VIPR's "unsplit" step turns a bound proved on both sides of a disjunction into a globally valid inequality, and a linear-combination step adds the component bounds. VeriPB derivations are DAG-like in the same way. The separation is tree-like against DAG-like, which is the standard one.

**Verdict.** KNOWN. **Confidence:** high.

**How to sharpen it into something new.**
1. Leave the disjoint case. Prove size bounds for LP-bounded AND/OR (or tree-decomposition) certificates on weakly coupled chains, in terms of pseudo-tree depth and of how much frustration crosses each separator. Chain molecules give a natural family. No lower bounds for AND/OR branch-and-bound with local-polytope bounding were found. The same question for approximate partition-function certificates (trees of the `Z*_epsilon` type with and without a join rule) is also open as far as this search shows; proof systems were found for exact counting only.
2. Characterise the minimal OR-tree certificate for heterogeneous unions exactly: fooling-number product as the lower bound, slack accounting where fooling numbers equal 1. This would connect the product bound to the Jeroslow and Chvátal knapsack constructions.

The competition's certified tracks require VeriPB proofs, with a "100 GB limit for the size of OPT/UNSAT proofs and a 5 h limit for the verification by VeriPB" ([PB25 page](http://www.cril.univ-artois.fr/PB25/)) `[V]`.

**Known trade-offs between witness size and checking time.**
- Hints remove search from the checker. L. Cruz-Filipe, M. J. H. Heule, W. A. Hunt, M. Kaufmann, P. Schneider-Kamp, "Efficient certified RAT verification", CADE 2017, [doi:10.1007/978-3-319-63046-5_14](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-63046-5_14) `[V-abs]`. That the hinted format trades a larger proof for checking without unit propagation is `[unverified-from-memory]`. The same idea appears as the VeriPB kernel format that CakePB consumes, and as the "RUP with hints" proposal above.
- Checking can dominate solving: 43 times in the median for certified MaxSAT branch-and-bound, with 30% of proofs not checked within limits. For exact SCIP with VIPR the reported overhead stays below the solving time.
- Kernel-level checking costs more again: roughly 200 times CakePB on the PBLean benchmark.
- Shrinking a tree certificate is hard. G. Muñoz, J. Paat, Á. S. Xavier, "Compressing branch-and-bound trees", Math. Program. 210:669-694 (2024), [doi:10.1007/s10107-024-02080-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-024-02080-5), [arXiv:2211.02727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02727) `[V]`: the tree compression problem is NP-complete (Theorem 1); some trees cannot be compressed below `(2^n - 1)/n` nodes (Theorem 2); exact compression reduced realistic trees by 35.2% on average. Finding a smallest tree is NP-hard: M. Gläser, M. E. Pfetsch, "On computing small variable disjunction branch-and-bound trees", Math. Program. 206:145-173 (2023), [doi:10.1007/s10107-023-01968-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-023-01968-y) `[V-abs]`.
- General framework: R. M. McConnell, K. Mehlhorn, S. Näher, P. Schweitzer, "Certifying algorithms", Comput. Sci. Rev. 5(2):119-161 (2011), [doi:10.1016/j.cosrev.2010.09.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cosrev.2010.09.009) `[V-abs]`.

**Verdict.** KNOWN landscape. The gap is specific: native certificates and a verified checker for cost-function-network and MAP lower bounds, and for the OSPREY-type DEE/A* pipeline. NO MATCH FOUND for either. **Confidence:** high for the landscape, medium for the gap (a cost-function network can already be encoded to pseudo-Boolean form and certified with existing tools, so the gap is about native witnesses and efficiency).

**How to sharpen it into something new.**
1. Define native witnesses and prove their checking cost. A reparameterisation is a linear-size witness for a local-polytope bound and is checked in one pass with exact arithmetic. A DEE step is witnessed by a pair `(r, t)` and checked by one Goldstein evaluation. A thermal-dominance step is witnessed by `(r, t, gamma)`. Compare size and checking time against the pseudo-Boolean encoding of the same run. The 43-times figure above is the number to beat.
2. Prove a hint trade-off theorem for LP-bounded search: witness with one gauge per leaf (large, linear-time check) against witness without gauges (small, checker must solve an LP), with delta-encoding of gauges along the tree as the intermediate point. A machine-checked checker in Lean 4 would sit beside LeanVIPR and PBLean and would be the first for this problem class.

## C5. Complexity of exact single-site dominance

**Claim as checked.** Exact single-site dominance checking is linear-time for pairwise energies (Goldstein's test is exact) and coNP-hard once 3-body terms are present, because the difference energy is then a pairwise model on the neighbours.

**Closest prior art.**

1. Exactness for pairwise models. Shekhovtsov 2014, section 4.1, and Shekhovtsov, Swoboda, Savchynskyy 2018, Example 2.1 (C1 items 1 and 3) `[V]`: the energy change is "additive in neighbors, so that it can be verified for all `x_N(u)`". de Givry et al. 2013, section 3 (C1 item 5) `[V]`: the Goldstein/pcost test "is equivalent to soft neighborhood substitutability when `Γ(x)` is separable ... and `α < k`", with complexity `O(q d^(r-1))` per dominance check for arity `r`, domain size `d` and `q` cost functions on the variable.
2. Hardness in general. Lecoutre et al. 2012 (C1 item 4) `[V-abs]`: "SNS is coNP-hard". de Givry et al. 2013, footnote 3 `[V]`: "Enforcing complete soft neighborhood substitutability is co-NP hard as soon as `k = +∞`". Which feature drives the hardness proof (non-separable scopes or bounded addition) could not be checked without the full text of Lecoutre et al.
3. Hardness of verifying improving maps. Shekhovtsov 2014 `[V]`: "the verification of the improving property is NP-hard: in case of binary variables it includes NP-hard decision problem of whether a partial assignment is an autarky"; Shekhovtsov 2016 `[V]` repeats this for the quadratic pseudo-Boolean case. These concern multi-variable maps in pairwise models.
4. Practice with 3-body terms. LuCore et al. 2015 (C2 item 7): the nested-min criterion is a relaxation, since the minimiser chosen for neighbour `k` may differ between the terms of different `j`. Pair ("doubles") criteria and generalised DEE stay polynomial because they remain pairwise: L. L. Looger, H. W. Hellinga, "Generalized dead-end elimination algorithms make large-scale protein side-chain structure prediction tractable", J. Mol. Biol. 307(1):429-445 (2001), [doi:10.1006/jmbi.2000.4424](https://doi.org/10.1006/jmbi.2000.4424) `[V-abs]`.
5. How the protein-design literature words it. M. A. Hallen, B. R. Donald, "Protein design by provable algorithms", Commun. ACM 62(10):76-84 (2019), [doi:10.1145/3338124](https://doi.org/10.1145/3338124), PMC6788629 `[V]`: the exact condition (their Eq. 3) "is as difficult as finding the GMEC directly. But the sum of minima is always a lower bound for the minimum of a sum, so we obtain the following sufficient condition" (their Eq. 4, Goldstein's test).

**Reviewer's remarks.**
- For finite pairwise energies on a full product domain, Eq. 3 and Eq. 4 of the Communications article coincide: each summand depends on a different residue, so the minimum of the sum is the sum of the minima. The cautious wording becomes accurate when pruned pairs or steric filters make the conformation space non-product. Exact dominance over the remaining conformations then contains constraint satisfaction and is coNP-hard in general, even for pairwise energies. The claim's first half is correct but must state the product-domain hypothesis.
- The 3-body reduction is immediate. Given a pairwise model `g` on the neighbours, set `T(r, y_j, y_k) = g_jk(y_j, y_k)` and `T(t, ., .) = 0`; then `t` dominates `r` iff `min g >= 0`.
- The hardness is a property of the link graph of site `i` (the graph on its neighbours induced by the 3-body terms through `i`). For chain-local terms `T_{i-1,i,i+1}` the link graph is a short path, and exact dominance is linear-time by dynamic programming. The coNP-hardness does not bite for the chain molecules MetricChain targets.

**Verdict.** First half KNOWN. Second half KNOWN-IN-SPIRIT. **Confidence:** high and medium-high.

**How to sharpen it into something new.**
1. State a dichotomy by link-graph treewidth: exact single-site dominance with `k`-body terms is solvable in time exponential only in the treewidth of the link hypergraph of the site, and coNP-hard for unrestricted link graphs. The tractability of bounded-treewidth minimisation is standard, so the content is in the formulation for DEE and in the corollary that chain-local 3-body models keep exact, cheap dominance. This was not found stated.
2. Identify the best polynomial test in the hard case: the local-polytope LP on the difference model. Prove that it dominates the nested-min rule of LuCore et al., and give the instances where it is strictly stronger.

## 6. Baselines: the strongest contemporary provable algorithms

- **OSPREY 3 (2018) is the reference platform for provable design with continuous flexibility.** M. A. Hallen et al., J. Comput. Chem. 39(30):2494-2507, [doi:10.1002/jcc.25522](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcc.25522) `[V]`. DEE/iMinDEE followed by A*; EPIC and LUTE surrogates; DEEPer and CATS for backbone motion; K* and BBK*; GPU minimisation. Guarantees are relative to the input model.
- **Continuous side chains: iMinDEE/A* (2012), then LUTE (2017).** iMinDEE enumerates in order of a pairwise-minimised lower bound and proves the minimised optimum. LUTE replaces the continuous, possibly non-pairwise energy by a fitted tuple expansion and optimises that exactly; the fit residual is empirical. (C2 items 2 and 5.)
- **Partition functions per sequence: K* (2005, 2008).** A* gap-free enumeration with an `epsilon`-approximation stopping rule and the `p*` bound for pruned conformations. (C3 items 3 and 4.)
- **Multi-sequence and region bounds: BBK* (2018), MARK* (2020), EWAK*/FRIES (2020).** BBK* runs "in time sublinear in the number of sequences". ## 7. Where novelty most plausibly sits

- Not in C1, C4(i) or the first half of C5 as stated. Each is either a short corollary or already a theorem.
- In C2, as a query-model optimality theorem for pruning intervals and a theory of envelopes (closure, certified construction for learned or truncated residuals).
- In C3, as a certificate-bearing pruning rule for partition functions that does not scale with the number of pruned conformations, and its refinement with marginal information.
- In C4(ii), as native certificates and a Lean 4 checker for reparameterisation bounds, DEE steps and thermal-dominance steps, with a measured comparison against pseudo-Boolean proof logging.
- In C5, as a link-graph dichotomy, with the practical corollary that chain-local 3-body terms keep exact dominance cheap.
