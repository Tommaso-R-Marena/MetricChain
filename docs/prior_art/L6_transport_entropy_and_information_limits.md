# L6 prior-art check: transport-entropy duality, extremal residuals, dependent references, certified free-energy perturbation, thermal contact, certification as quantization, weighted sphere covering, type covering

Literature check performed on 2026-11-24 for the MetricChain program. Setting: a finite configuration space `X` (typically a product `Sigma^n`); an envelope `d` on `X`, that is a quasi-metric with `d(x,x) = 0` and the triangle inequality, symmetry not required (typically the weighted Hamming distance `d(x,y) = sum_i c_i 1[x_i != y_i]`); a reference Gibbs measure `mu ∝ exp(-beta f)`; an unknown residual `g` with `g(x) - g(y) <= d(x,y)` ("1-Lipschitz"); the target `mu_g ∝ mu exp(-beta g)`; and `ln(Z_g/Z) = ln E_mu exp(-beta g)`. Covers claims T1 to T8.

## T1. Exact worst-case free-energy gap as a transport-entropy conjugate

**Claim as checked.** For every probability measure `mu` and `beta >= 0`,

    sup_{g 1-Lipschitz} ln E_mu exp(beta (g - E_mu g))  =  sup_nu [ beta W_d(nu, mu) - KL(nu || mu) ].

**Reviewer's derivation (three lines, to fix conventions).** Donsker-Varadhan: `ln E_mu exp(h) = sup_nu [E_nu h - KL(nu||mu)]`. Put `h = beta(g - E_mu g)`, take the supremum over `g`, exchange the two suprema, and use Kantorovich-Rubinstein, `sup_g (E_nu g - E_mu g) = W_d(nu, mu)`. On a finite set nothing else is needed.

**Closest prior art.**
- Bobkov and Gotze, "Exponential integrability and transportation cost related to logarithmic Sobolev inequalities", J. Funct. Anal. 163(1):1-28 (1999), doi:10.1006/jfan.1998.3326. `[U]` for the primary text (publisher page and author copy not reachable). The statement is quoted as follows. Djellout-Guillin-Wu 2004, Theorem 1.1: `mu` satisfies `T1(C)` on `(E,d)` if and only if `int exp(lambda(F - <F>_mu)) dmu <= exp(lambda^2 C ||F||_Lip^2 / 2)` for every Lipschitz `F` and every real `lambda` `[V]`. Raginsky-Sason 2013, Theorem 3.4.3, attributes it to "[Bobkov-Gotze, Theorem 3.1]" and gives a full proof `[V]`. Gozlan-Leonard 2007 cites "Theorem 1.3 and statement (1.7)" of the same paper `[V]`. The two numberings were not reconciled here.
- Gozlan and Leonard, "A large deviation approach to some transportation cost inequalities", Probab. Theory Relat. Fields 139:235-283 (2007), doi:10.1007/s00440-006-0045-y, arXiv:math/0510601. `[V]` Theorem 3.7: for convex increasing `alpha` with `alpha(0) = 0`, `alpha(T(nu)) <= H(nu|mu)` for all `nu` if and only if `Lambda_phi(s) <= alpha^*(s)` for all `s >= 0` and all test pairs, where `Lambda_phi` is the log-Laplace transform and `alpha^*` the monotone conjugate. Theorem 3.17 is the `T1` case with the class of bounded 1-Lipschitz functions. Definition 5.2 and the text after Remark 5.4: "the best transportation function is the left continuous version of the increasing function `t -> inf{H(nu|mu); T(nu) >= t}`". **Corollary 7.11(a): "The best transportation function in the class C is `Lambda_Phi^*`"**, with `Lambda_Phi(s) = sup over test pairs of Lambda_phi(s)`. Taking monotone conjugates on both sides (their Proposition 3.4) gives exactly the displayed identity. So the identity is in the literature in conjugate form.
- Gozlan and Leonard, "Transport inequalities. A survey", Markov Process. Related Fields 16:635-736 (2010), arXiv:1003.3852. `[V]` Theorem 3.2 (general lower semicontinuous cost `c`, pairs `u (+) v <= c`), Corollary 3.3 (optimised forms with `P_c f` and `Q_c g`), Corollary 3.4 (for a lower semicontinuous distance: `alpha(T_d) <= H` if and only if `int exp(s f) dmu <= exp(s int f dmu + alpha^*(s))` for all 1-Lipschitz `f`). The proof of Theorem 3.2 contains the exchange of suprema ("optimizing over `nu`") that gives the identity; the identity itself is not displayed there. The survey says a variant is in Villani's textbook, Theorem 5.26.
- Bobkov, Houdre and Tetali, "The subgaussian constant and concentration inequalities", Israel J. Math. 156:255-283 (2006), doi:10.1007/BF02773835. `[V]` (read in an online copy that appears to be the extended manuscript; numbering may differ from the journal). They define `L_G(t) = sup_f E exp(t(f - Ef))` over Lipschitz `f` and `sigma^2(G) = sup_{t>0} ln L_G(t) / (t^2/2)`, and state: "The subgaussian constant is also the optimal value in a transport inequality on `(V,d)`, `W_1^2(pi, nu) <= 2 sigma^2 D(nu||pi)`". This is the Gaussian-constant version of T1 stated as an equality of optimal constants.
- The left-hand side has a name: the Laplace functional of the metric measure space. Ledoux, *The Concentration of Measure Phenomenon*, AMS Math. Surveys Monogr. 89 (2001), doi:10.1090/surv/089, Section 1.6. `[U]` for the book; the definition and the reference to Section 1.6 and Proposition 1.15 are quoted in Brunel and Serres, arXiv:2303.01144, Section 2.1 `[V]`.
- Textbook proofs of the equivalence: Raginsky and Sason, "Concentration of measure inequalities in information theory, communications, and coding", Found. Trends Commun. Inf. Theory 10(1-2) (2013), doi:10.1561/0100000064, arXiv:1212.4663, Section 3.4.3 `[V]`; Ledoux, "Concentration of measure and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities" (lecture notes, Section 3.3) `[V]` for the text, `[U]` for the publication details (Seminaire de Probabilites XXXIII, 1999). Villani, *Optimal Transport, Old and New* (2009), Theorems 5.10, 5.26 and 22.10 `[U]`.
- The same variational formula as a UQ tool: Dupuis, Katsoulakis, Pantazis, Plechac 2016, eq. (2.8), `Lambda~_{P,f}(c) = sup_{Q << P} { c(E_Q f - E_P f) - R(Q||P) }` `[V]`.

**Question (a): is the identity stated, or only the equivalence of Gaussian bounds?** More than the Gaussian equivalence is stated: the equivalence holds for every convex deviation function (Gozlan-Leonard 2007, Theorems 3.7 and 3.17; survey Theorem 3.2 and Corollary 3.4), and the optimal convex deviation function is identified as the conjugate of the Laplace functional (Corollary 7.11(a)). The identity in the exact form of the claim was not found displayed verbatim; it is that corollary read through a Legendre transform.

**Question (b): Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality for quasi-metric costs.** Yes.
- Rachev, "The Monge-Kantorovich mass transference problem and its stochastic applications", Theory Probab. Appl. 29(4):647-676 (1984), doi:10.1137/1129093. `[V]` (page image read), eqs. (2.10)-(2.11): for `U` compact and `c` an arbitrary continuous cost function, "Levin and Milyutin proved the dual relation" between the Kantorovich-Rubinstein transshipment functional (measures `Q` with `Q(A x U) - Q(U x A) = P_1(A) - P_2(A)`) and `sup { int f d(P_1 - P_2) : f(x) - f(y) <= c(x,y) }`. The constraint is one-sided, with no symmetry assumed. Primary source: Levin and Milyutin, Russian Math. Surveys 34(3):1-78 (1979), doi:10.1070/RM1979v034n03ABEH003996 `[U]`. The same page of Rachev's survey reports a theorem of Levin on when the transport and transshipment values coincide; the condition as printed there could not be interpreted with confidence and is not relied on.
- When `c` satisfies the triangle inequality and `c(x,x) = 0`, transshipment and transport coincide and the dual potential pair collapses to one potential. Reviewer's derivation on a finite set: from a Kantorovich pair `(phi, psi)` with `phi(x) + psi(y) <= c(x,y)` put `u(x) = min_y [c(x,y) - psi(y)]`; then `u >= phi`, `-u >= psi` (take `y = x`), and `u(x) - u(x') <= c(x,x')` by the triangle inequality, so `(u, -u)` is feasible and at least as good. This is the usual proof for metrics (Villani, Particular Case 5.4 `[U]`) and never uses symmetry. In the Gozlan-Leonard survey the same fact reads `P_c f = f` for `f` with `f(x) - f(y) <= c(x,y)`, so their Corollary 3.3, which is stated for general lower semicontinuous costs with `c(x,x) = 0`, already gives T1 for quasi-metrics `[V]` for the corollary; the specialisation is mine.
- Quasi-metric versions in the literature: Goubault-Larrecq, "Complete quasi-metrics for hyperspaces, continuous valuations, and previsions", arXiv:1707.03784, Lemma 11.44 and Theorem 11.55, "a form of linear duality theorem, in the style of Kantorovich and Rubinshtein, for quasi-metric spaces" `[V]`; Jevtic, Jelic, Zivaljevic, "Cyclohedron and Kantorovich-Rubinstein polytopes", Arnold Math. J. 4:87-112 (2018), doi:10.1007/s40598-018-0083-4, arXiv:1703.06612, Section 2.4 (Kantorovich-Rubinstein polytopes of quasi-metrics) `[V]`.
- Reviewer's numerics: primal and one-potential dual LPs agree to `7e-16` on 200 random quasi-metrics; the identity of T1 was confirmed on six random 4-point quasi-metric instances by exhaustive vertex enumeration against multi-start maximisation (agreement to `4e-10`).

**Corrections to the wording.**
1. *Orientation.* With `g(x) - g(y) <= d(x,y)` the cost pairs `x ~ nu` with the first argument of `d`. The free-energy gap uses `exp(-beta g)`, and `-g` is 1-Lipschitz for the transposed envelope `d^T(x,y) = d(y,x)`, so the worst-case gap `ln E_mu exp(-beta g) + beta E_mu g` equals `sup_nu [beta W_d(mu, nu) - KL(nu||mu)]`. In the numerical check the two orientations differ substantially (for example 0.494 against 1.620).
2. *What the identity determines.* The right-hand side is the monotone conjugate of the profile `J(t) = inf{KL(nu||mu) : W_d(nu,mu) >= t}`. The profile need not be convex, so the Laplace functional determines only its convex envelope (this is the content of Gozlan-Leonard's distinction between the best transportation function and the best convex one).
3. *Beyond finite sets* one needs an exponential moment of `d(x_0, .)` and bounded Lipschitz test functions (hypotheses of Gozlan-Leonard 2007, Theorem 3.17).

**Verdict.** KNOWN. Textbook duality; the quasi-metric case is a routine consequence of Kantorovich duality for general costs. Nothing here can be presented as new mathematics.

**Confidence.** High.

**How the claim would have to be sharpened to be new.**
1. *An anchored version.* With `g` evaluated on a finite set `A`, the admissible class is the Lipschitz class pinned on `A`. The corresponding exact worst-case gap is the conjugate of a transport cost whose potentials are pinned on `A`. No source for this constrained duality surfaced, but it was not searched for specifically.
2. *Exact evaluation for non-product references.* The identity is only useful once one side can be computed. For product references this is T2 (known). For tree-structured references it is open as far as this check found (T3).
3. *A machine-checked statement for quasi-metrics with the orientation made explicit.* This would be a formalization contribution, not a mathematical one; whether proof-assistant libraries already contain Bobkov-Gotze duality was not checked here.

## T2. Extremal structure for product measures and weighted Hamming distance

**Claim as checked.** For `mu = (x)_i mu_i` and `d(x,y) = sum_i c_i 1[x_i != y_i]`, the supremum in T1 is attained by an additive two-level function `g = sum_i c_i 1[x_i in A_i]`, equivalently by a product measure `nu`, with value `sum_i max_{A_i} ln(p_i exp(beta c_i (1 - p_i)) + (1 - p_i) exp(-beta c_i p_i))`, `p_i = mu_i(A_i)`.

**Closest prior art.**
- **Product step (additive functions are extremal).** Bobkov-Houdre-Tetali 2006 `[V]` (extended manuscript): "for `G^n` the Cartesian product graph (equipped with an `l1`-type metric), `sigma^2(G^n) = n sigma^2(G)`" (their (1.6)), and, with `L_G(t) = sup_f E exp(t(f - Ef))`: "Note also that `L_{G^n}(t) = L_G(t)^n`, for all `t`, (see [reference] for a proof)". This is an exact statement for a fixed measure. The tensorisation is attributed to Alon, Boppana and Spencer, "An asymptotic isoperimetric inequality", Geom. Funct. Anal. 8(3):411-436 (1998), doi:10.1007/s000390050062 (`[U]` for the primary text) by Yancey, "Probabilistic and geometrical applications to graph theory", arXiv:1705.09725, who reproduces it with proof as Theorem 2.2 `[V]`: for graphs `G`, `H`, `sigma^2_{G box H} = sigma^2_G + sigma^2_H`; the proof shows `L_{G box H}(t) = L_G(t) + L_H(t)` for the log-moment functions, the lower bound being attained by the additive function `l(u,v) = f(u) + f'(v)` and the upper bound following from conditioning on one coordinate. The general metric-measure form is Ledoux's Proposition 1.15 (`Lambda_{(X_1,...,X_n)} <= prod Lambda_{X_i}` for the `l1` product metric) `[U]`, quoted as Lemma 2.1 in Brunel-Serres, arXiv:2303.01144 `[V]`. The reverse inequality is trivial because additive functions are Lipschitz for the `l1` metric and their moment generating function factorises.
- **Single-site step (two-level functions are extremal), for an arbitrary fixed measure.** Bobkov-Houdre-Tetali 2006, section "Complete graph" `[V]`. For `V` with the metric `d(x,y) = 1{x != y}` and an arbitrary probability measure `mu`: "The class `F_0` of such functions is compact and convex, and the functional `f -> L(t,f)` is convex. Hence, it attains its maximum on `F_0` at some extremal 'point' of `F_0`. But the extremal functions in `F_0` are just indicator functions `f = 1_A`. Thus `sup_f L(t,f) = sup_A L(t, 1_A)`." Their proposition then gives `sigma^2(K_v, mu) = (p - q) / (2(log p - log q))`, `p = inf{mu(A) : mu(A) >= 1/2}`. Their Proposition 2.1 is the two-point case and Proposition 2.3 the weighted cube `mu_p^n`: `sigma^2 = n(p - q) / (2(log p - log q))`.
- **Worst case over all product measures.** Rio, "On McDiarmid's concentration inequality", Electron. Commun. Probab. 18(44):1-11 (2013), doi:10.1214/ECP.v18-2659 `[V]` (re-read in this session). Lemma 4.1: `ln E exp(tZ - tEZ) <= l(Delta_1 t) + ... + l(Delta_n t)`, `l(t) = sup_{r in [0,1]} [ln(1 + r(e^t - 1)) - tr]`, maximiser `r_t = (e^t - t - 1)/(t(e^t - 1))`; Remark 2.2: the conjugate of `l` is Vajda's information function. The claim's bracket is `gamma(p_i, beta c_i)` in Rio's notation, so the fixed-measure value is `sum_i max_{A_i} gamma(mu_i(A_i), beta c_i)` and Rio's bound is its supremum over all measures.
- **Dual side (transport-entropy profile of a fixed measure for the trivial metric).** Ordentlich and Weinberger, "A distribution dependent refinement of Pinsker's inequality", IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 51(5):1836-1840 (2005), doi:10.1109/TIT.2005.846407 `[U]`; quoted in Raginsky-Sason, Remark 3.4.6, with `phi(p) = ln((1-p)/p)/(1-2p)` and the balance coefficient `pi_P = max_A min{P(A), 1 - P(A)}` `[V]`. Berend, Harremoes, Kontorovich, "Minimum KL-divergence on complements of L1 balls", IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 60(6):3172-3177 (2014), doi:10.1109/TIT.2014.2301446, arXiv:1206.6544 `[V]`: they study `D*(v, Q) = inf{D(P||Q) : V(P,Q) >= v}` for a fixed `Q`; Lemma 3 shows the minimiser for a given set `A` is the two-level reweighting `p* = (a 1_A + b 1_{A^c}) q`; Theorem 1 gives `D*(v,Q) = KL_2(beta - v/2, beta)` for unbalanced `Q` and small `v`. The two-level reweighting is the dual optimiser `nu_i ∝ mu_i exp(beta c_i 1_{A_i})` of the claim.
- **Product optimiser and tensorisation of the dual.** Gozlan-Leonard survey, Proposition 1.8 (inf-convolution tensorisation of `alpha(T_c) <= H`) `[V]`; Raginsky-Sason, Theorem 3.4.4 (Bernoulli(p) product: Kearns-Saul constant through `T1` and tensorisation) and Theorem 3.4.5 (Marton's coupling inequality, with a pointer to Boucheron-Lugosi-Massart, Theorem 8.2) `[V]`. Marton, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 32(3):445-446 (1986), doi:10.1109/TIT.1986.1057176, and Ann. Probab. 24(2):857-866 (1996), doi:10.1214/aop/1039639365: records `[V]`, texts `[U]`.
- **Related refinements.** Bentkus, "On Hoeffding's inequalities", Ann. Probab. 32(2):1650-1673 (2004), doi:10.1214/009117904000000360, arXiv:math/0410159 `[V-abs]`: tails of bounded-difference martingales dominated by a constant times the tail of an i.i.d. Bernoulli sum (the tail-level counterpart of two-level extremality, with a constant). Owhadi et al., "Optimal uncertainty quantification", SIAM Rev. 55(2):271-345 (2013), doi:10.1137/10080782X `[V-abs]` here (L2 read Theorem 5.2: at the tail level the extremal function is not additive in general). Sammer and Tetali, "Concentration on the discrete torus using transportation", Combin. Probab. Comput. 18(5):835-860 (2009), doi:10.1017/S0963548309990319 `[V-abs]`. Maurer, "Thermodynamics and concentration", Bernoulli 18(2):434-454 (2012), doi:10.3150/10-BEJ341 `[V-abs]`. McDiarmid 1989 and Boucheron-Lugosi-Massart (2013), Theorem 6.2: `[U]`.

**Is exact attainment for a FIXED product measure stated?** Yes. `L_{G^n}(t) = L_G(t)^n` (Bobkov-Houdre-Tetali; Alon-Boppana-Spencer) is a fixed-measure identity whose proof exhibits the additive extremal function, and the single-site reduction to indicators is proved by Bobkov-Houdre-Tetali for an arbitrary measure with the trivial metric. The published statements are for powers of one graph with one measure (or two graphs with uniform measures); the case of different factors `mu_i` and weights `c_i` uses the same two-line argument and is covered by Ledoux's Proposition 1.15.

**Reviewer's numerics.** On eight random product instances (shapes 3x3, 2x2x2, 3x2x2, 4x3; `beta` = 0.7 and 2.5) a multi-start search over the full bounded-difference polytope matched the additive two-level formula to `1e-15`.

**Verdict.** KNOWN (a known specialised result: Alon-Boppana-Spencer tensorisation and the Bobkov-Houdre-Tetali complete-graph argument; Rio 2013 for the supremum over measures). The claim's heterogeneous form is a routine consequence. This revises L2 item C7.3, which recorded that attainment was implicit in Rio's proof and that no explicit statement, in particular none for a fixed product measure, had been found.

**Confidence.** High.

**How the claim would have to be sharpened to be new.**
1. *General site envelopes.* For a site-additive quasi-metric `d = sum_i delta_i(x_i, y_i)` with arbitrary (asymmetric, many-valued) `delta_i`, the product step still holds verbatim, but the single-site extremal functions are vertices of a Lipschitz polytope and are no longer indicators. Bobkov-Houdre-Tetali reduce the maximisation to extremal functions of the Lipschitz polytope `[V]` and show that for an extremal function the tight edges contain a spanning tree (as summarised in Yancey, arXiv:1705.09725, Section 2.3 `[V]`). A closed description for the envelopes the program actually uses would be a modest extension.
2. *Stability.* A quantitative statement that a residual whose gap is within `eta` of the worst case is close to an additive two-level function was not found (not searched specifically).
3. *Dependent references.* This is where the structure genuinely changes; see T3.

## T3. Dependent references

**Claim as checked.** `T1` and bounded-difference bounds for Gibbs measures under Dobrushin's uniqueness condition and for Markov chains, with precise constants; whether an exact attained worst-case formula exists for tree-structured or Markov references; whether a variance-aware version exists that is expressed through conditional variances computable by transfer matrices.

**Constants.** `C` is the Dobrushin interdependence matrix, `C_ij = sup` of the Wasserstein (for the trivial metric: total variation) distance between the conditional laws at site `i` when only site `j` is changed, per unit distance at `j`. The last column rewrites each result as `gap <= beta^2 K sum_i c_i^2 / 8` (reviewer's arithmetic; where the source prints a tail bound, `K` is read off the sub-Gaussian variance proxy).

| Source | Hypothesis | Statement as printed | Normalised form |
|---|---|---|---|

## T5. Thermal contact theorem

**Claim as checked.** `sup_g mu_g({x}) = 1 / sum_y exp(-beta(f(y) - f(x) + d(y,x)))`, the supremum being over admissible `g`. This is item P5 of the companion file L5, which reaches the same verdict and cites, in addition, the bounds on a softmax output from bounds on logit differences used in neural-network verification (Wei et al. 2023; not read here). The zero-temperature counterpart is the contact-set theorem of the theory report.

**Reviewer's derivation.** `mu_g(x) = 1 / sum_y exp(-beta(f(y) - f(x) + g(y) - g(x)))`. Each term decreases in `g(y) - g(x)`, which is at most `d(y,x)`. All these upper bounds are attained simultaneously by `g = d(., x)`, which is admissible by the triangle inequality (McShane). Symmetrically `inf_g mu_g({x}) = 1 / sum_y exp(-beta(f(y) - f(x) - d(x,y)))`, attained by `g = -d(x, .)`. The class is convex and `g -> mu_g(x)` is continuous, so every value in between is attained. Numerical check: no violation on 200 random quasi-metric instances.

**Closest prior art.**

**Confidence.** Medium. The statement is simple enough that absence from the literature is hard to establish and of limited significance.

**How the claim would have to be sharpened to be new.**

## T6. Certification as quantization; rate-distortion converse

**Claim as checked.** With `g` evaluated exactly on a finite set `A` and slack `s(a,y) = d(a,y) - g(a) + g(y) >= 0`, the upper end of the certified range of the partition function is `Z_hi(A) = Z E_pi exp(beta s(A,Y))`, `s(A,y) = min_{a in A} s(a,y)`, `pi` the true Gibbs measure. A certificate of `ln Z` to accuracy `eps` therefore forces `E_pi s(A,Y) <= eps/beta`, hence `|A| >= exp(R(eps/beta))` with `R` the rate-distortion function of `pi` for the distortion `s`; for weighted Hamming envelopes the Shannon lower bound gives `|A| >= exp(H(pi) - n h(delta) - n delta ln(k-1))`.

**Reviewer's check of the statement.** The lower McShane envelope is `L(y) = max_a [g(a) - d(a,y)] = g(y) - s(A,y)`, so `Z_hi = sum_y exp(-beta(f + L)) = Z E_pi exp(beta s(A,Y))`. This is Zwanzig's perturbation identity between the true potential and its envelope. Jensen gives `ln Z_hi - ln Z >= beta E_pi s(A,Y)`. The quantizer `q(y) = argmin_a s(a,y)` has `|A|` values, so `ln|A| >= H(q(Y)) >= I(Y; q(Y)) >= R(eps/beta)`. Numerically: identity confirmed to `1.5e-11` on 40 instances; the converse held in 160 of 160 cases with a certified dual lower bound on `R`, and `exp(R)` recovered 26% to 97% of the optimal anchor count (mean 68%).

**Relation to L4.** Items C8 and C9 of L4 cover the same question and cite the same core sources (Novak 1988; Dereich-Muller-Gronbach-Ritter and its journal version; Baran-Demaine-Katz; Holzmuller-Bach), with the same verdict. What this section adds: the inequality between quantization error and the distortion-rate function read in Dereich-Scheutzow, the general converse for real-valued distortions read in Polyanskiy-Wu, sampling-oracle lower bounds for partition functions, and the two corrections to the wording below.

**Closest prior art, by layer.**

**Verdict.** PARTIALLY KNOWN.
- "The worst-case error of certified integration over a Lipschitz class with nodes `A` is the `L^1` quantization error of the measure" is a known specialised result (Sukharev; Novak 1988; Dereich-Muller-Gronbach-Ritter 2006, Theorem 1).
- "Quantization error is at least the distortion-rate function", the one-shot converse and the Shannon lower bound are textbook.
- The identity `Z_hi(A) = Z E_pi exp(beta s(A,Y))` is Zwanzig's identity applied to the envelope: routine.
- The instance-dependent statement (the certificate size for the partition function of a particular landscape is bounded below by the rate-distortion function of its own Gibbs measure under the slack distortion): NO MATCH FOUND. It is a routine consequence of the textbook converse once the identity is written down, but the formulation appears to be new.

**Confidence.** Medium. Information-based complexity and quantization are large literatures; the searches combined "rate distortion" with "information-based complexity", "numerical integration", "partition function", "free energy" and "certificate" and found nothing closer than the items above.

**Corrections to the wording.**
1. The Shannon-lower-bound form needs a hypothesis that links slack to Hamming distance, for example that `g` is `kappa`-Lipschitz with `kappa < 1`, so that `s >= (1 - kappa) c_min d_H` and `delta = eps / (n beta (1 - kappa) c_min)`. For an extremal `g` the slack can vanish along whole directions and no entropy bound of this form holds (compare the linear function in Baran-Demaine-Katz).
2. The Jensen step is lossy. The exact statement is that `ln Z_hi(A) - ln Z` is the logarithm of the quantization error of `pi` for the distortion `rho_beta(a,y) = exp(beta s(a,y))`. Hence the sharper converse `|A| >= exp(R_{pi, rho_beta}(e^eps))`, again by the general converse.

**How the claim would have to be sharpened to be new.**
1. Prove the matching direction: existence of a certificate of size `exp(R + o(n))` for product-like landscapes (random coding or the greedy cover of the Johnson-Stein-Lovasz theorem, see T7), so that the rate-distortion function of the Gibbs measure is the certificate complexity to first order in the exponent.
2. Give an instance-optimal adaptive algorithm with a competitive guarantee against the best certificate, in the sense of Baran-Demaine-Katz, for discrete landscapes and the exponential functional. Their logarithmic factor is unavoidable in one dimension; what replaces it here is open.
3. Treat both ends of the certified interval (the lower end involves the upper envelope and the transposed slack) and state the two-sided certificate complexity.

## T7. Sphere-covering bound with Boltzmann weights

**Claim as checked.** If every `y` has some `a in A` with `s(a,y) <= r(y)`, then for all `t >= 0`, `|A| >= sum_y exp(-t r(y)) / max_a sum_y exp(-t s(a,y))`.

**Reviewer's derivation.** `exp(-t r(y)) <= exp(-t s(a(y), y)) <= sum_{a in A} exp(-t s(a,y))`; sum over `y`. This is the Chernoff relaxation `1[s <= r] <= exp(t(r - s))` of a weighted counting bound. Numerically, on 60 random instances with point-dependent radii: weighted bound `<=` fractional-cover LP `<=` minimal cover in every case, with means 1.03, 3.57, 3.58. The bound is weak on small unstructured instances; its value lies in exponents for product structures.

**Relation to L4.** Item C4 of L4 analyses the same inequality (there for certified optimisation) and identifies it as the dual lower bound on a rate-distortion function at a trivial dual point, equivalently a weighted sphere-covering bound after a Chernoff step. This section agrees and adds two closer matches: the specialisation of Kostina-Verdu's Theorem 8, and Merhav's extended Kraft inequality for fixed-rate codes.

**Closest prior art.**
- *The exact bound of this type is a linear program.* Cullina and Kiyavash, "Generalized sphere-packing bounds on the size of codes for combinatorial channels", IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 62(8):4454-4465 (2016), doi:10.1109/TIT.2016.2565578; arXiv:1405.1464 (arXiv title: "Generalized sphere-packing and sphere-covering bounds ...") `[V]` for abstract and introduction: "These generalizations become especially important when the sizes of the error spheres are nonuniform. The best possible sphere-packing and sphere-covering bounds are solutions to linear programs"; simplified bounds are dual feasible points (the local degree bound of Kulkarni-Kiyavash). Fazeli, Vardy, Yaakobi, "Generalized sphere packing bound", arXiv:1401.6496: `[U]` (arXiv listing only). Lovasz, "On the ratio of optimal integral and fractional covers", Discrete Math. 13:383-390 (1975), doi:10.1016/0012-365X(75)90058-8: record `[V]`; the Johnson-Stein-Lovasz theorem (greedy cover within a logarithmic factor of the fractional optimum) is named in Csiszar 1998 as the modern source of the type covering lemma `[V]`.
- *The same bound in rate-distortion language, with an arbitrary reference measure.* Kostina and Verdu, "Fixed-length lossy compression in the finite blocklength regime", IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 58(6):3309-3338 (2012), doi:10.1109/TIT.2012.2186786, arXiv:1102.3944 `[V]`. Theorem 8: any `(M, d, eps)` code satisfies `M >= sup_Q inf_y beta_{1-eps}(P_X, Q) / Q[d(X,y) <= d]`, the supremum over all (also sigma-finite) `Q`; with `Q` uniform it "yields a lower bound to the minimum number of d-balls required to cover" a set of probability `1 - eps`. At `eps = 0` and for a source with full support this is the fractional-covering bound `M >= sup_Q Q(X) / max_a Q(S(a))` (my specialisation). T7 is this bound with `Q(y) ∝ exp(-t r(y))`, followed by a Chernoff bound on `Q(S(a))`. Their Theorem 7 is proved with the same exponential weighting: the d-tilted information satisfies `E exp(lambda* d - lambda* d(X,y) + j_X(X,d)) <= 1` for all `y` (Property 2), and `P_Y(B_d(x)) <= E exp(lambda* d - lambda* d(x,Y))` (their (24)-(25)).
- *Parametric lower bound on the rate-distortion function.* Csiszar, "On an extremum problem of information theory", Studia Sci. Math. Hungar. 9:57-71 (1974) `[U]`; quoted as Theorem 41 in Kostina-Verdu `[V]`: `R_X(d) = max { E alpha(X) - lambda d }` over `alpha >= 0`, `lambda >= 0` with `E exp(alpha(X) - lambda d(X,y)) <= 1` for all `y`. Gallager, *Information Theory and Reliable Communication* (1968), Theorem 9.4.1, is the finite-alphabet form `[U]`. Reviewer's derivation: with the distortion `s(a,y) - r(y)` at level 0, source law `Q`, `lambda = t` and `alpha(y) = -t r(y) - ln Q(y) - ln max_a sum_y' exp(-t s(a,y'))`, the constraint holds and `R_Q(0) >= H(Q) - t E_Q r - ln max_a sum_y exp(-t s(a,y))`; maximising over `Q` (Gibbs variational principle) gives exactly the logarithm of T7. So T7 is the maximum over sources of the standard parametric lower bound with a flat multiplier, combined with the general converse.
- *The same inequality as an extended Kraft inequality.* Merhav, "Refinements and generalizations of the Shannon lower bound via extensions of the Kraft inequality", arXiv:2512.11322 (December 2025) `[V]`. The method is "to upper bound the summation ... of an exponentiated negative linear combination of the code-length and the distortion incurred by each and every vector in the source space". Lemma 4 (fixed-rate codes, difference distortion `rho`): `int exp_2{-alpha n R - beta rho(u^n - psi_n(phi_n(u^n)))} du^n <= 2^{n(1-alpha)R} [int 2^{-beta rho(z)} dz]^n` for all `alpha >= 0`, `beta >= 0`. At `alpha = 0` this reads `M >= sum_u exp(-beta rho(u - u^)) / sum_z exp(-beta rho(z))`, which is T7 for a difference distortion (the denominator does not depend on the centre). Lemma 3 is the version for D-semifaithful codes. Antecedents named there: Campbell, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 19(1):68-73 (1973), doi:10.1109/TIT.1973.1054953; Merhav, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 41(4):1200-1202 (1995), doi:10.1109/18.391272: records `[V]`, texts `[U]`.
- *Point-dependent radius.* Absorbed by redefining the distortion as `s(a,y) - r(y)` at level zero. Kostina-Verdu assume `d >= 0`, Polyanskiy-Wu allow real values in the average-distortion converse; the counting argument needs no sign condition.
- *Landscape theory.* No statement relating a density of states to ball volumes through such a bound surfaced.

**Verdict.** KNOWN-IN-SPIRIT, close to KNOWN: a routine consequence (Chernoff relaxation) of the fractional-covering bound, obtained from Kostina-Verdu's Theorem 8 with a tilted reference measure, and identical to Merhav's Lemma 4 at `alpha = 0` in the difference-distortion case. The exact sentence with a general slack and point-dependent radius was not found.

**Confidence.** Medium-High.

**How the claim would have to be sharpened to be new.**
1. Present the hierarchy: T7 `<=` `exp(max_Q R_Q(0))` `<=` fractional cover `<=` minimal cover `<=` `(1 + ln max|S(a)|)` times the fractional cover. (The second inequality is a reviewer's derivation: from a fractional cover `x` of total weight `tau`, the test channel `P(a|y) ∝ x_a 1[y in S(a)]` has zero distortion and `I(Y;A) <= E_Q ln(tau / c(Y)) <= ln tau`, where `c(y) >= 1` is the fractional coverage of `y`. The last inequality is the Johnson-Stein-Lovasz bound.) On small unstructured instances the first two terms are far below the last two (means 1.03, 1.04, 3.65, 3.67 in a second run of 40 instances), because an average-distortion constraint lets credit at well-covered points pay for uncovered ones. Then determine for which product landscapes the chain is tight in the exponent: for identical sites the type covering lemma closes the gap between the rate-distortion term and the minimal cover (T8), and what remains is whether the flat multiplier of T7 loses in the exponent. That is a tightness theorem, not a bound.
2. Use general weights `u(y) exp(-t r(y))` and optimise `u`; this recovers the LP, and for tree-structured `f` the optimisation may be tractable by transfer matrices.

## T8. Type-covering asymptotics

**Claim as checked.** For `rho(a,y) = sum_i [c 1[a_i != y_i] - e(y_i)]`, the minimal size of `A` such that every `y` has some `a` with `rho(a,y) <= 0` grows like `exp(n max_P R_P)`, `R_P` the rate-distortion function at level 0 for the type `P`.

**The exact classical statement.** Csiszar, "The method of types", IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 44(6):2505-2523 (1998), doi:10.1109/18.720546 `[V]` (page 2515, read from the page image): "The usefulness for rate-distortion theory of partitioning n-length sequences into type classes was first demonstrated by Berger. He established the following lemma, called the type covering lemma in [Csiszar-Korner]. *Lemma VI.1: Given finite sets `X`, `Y` and a nonnegative function `d(x,y)` on `X x Y`, for `P in P_n(X)` and `D >= 0` let `N_n(P,D)` denote the minimum number of 'd-balls of radius D', `B(y,D) = {x : d(x,y) <= D}`, needed to cover the type class `T_P^n`. Then `max_{P in P_n(X)} | (1/n) log N_n(P,D) - R(P,D) | -> 0` as `n -> inf`, where `R(P,D) = min I(X ^ Y)` subject to `P_X = P`, `E d(X,Y) <= D`.*" The text continues: "This lemma is seen today as a consequence of a simple general result about coverings known as the Johnson-Stein-Lovasz theorem ... **An immediate consequence of Lemma VI.1 is that the minimum (asymptotic) rate of source block codes admitting the reproduction of each `x in X^n` by some `y in Y^n` with distortion `d(x,y) <= D`, is equal to `max_P R(P,D)`.** Berger also used this lemma to derive the rate-distortion theorem for arbitrarily varying sources."

**Other sources.**
- Berger, *Rate Distortion Theory* (1971) `[U]`; Berger, "The source coding game", IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 17(1):71-76 (1971), doi:10.1109/TIT.1971.1054577: record `[V]`, text `[U]`. Its Lemma 1 is quoted as Lemma 1.1 (Type Covering) in Palaiyanur, Chang, Sahai, "The source coding game with a cheating switcher", arXiv:0712.2870 `[V]`: for a fixed type `p` and `eps > 0`, for `n` large there is a codebook with fewer than `exp(n(R(p,D) + eps))` codewords covering `T_p^n` within distortion `D`. They take the union over at most `(n+1)^{|X|}` types to obtain `max_p R(p,D)` for an arbitrarily varying source with a non-causal adversary. Their distortion maps into `[0, d*]`.
- Csiszar and Korner, *Information Theory: Coding Theorems for Discrete Memoryless Systems* (1981; second edition 2011): the lemma number (9.1 in the second edition, in the chapter "A covering lemma and the error exponent in source coding") is `[U]`; only the chapter title appeared in a search snippet.
- Refinements named by Csiszar 1998: Marton's error exponent for source coding with a fidelity criterion, and Yu-Speed, Linder-Lugosi-Zeger, Merhav, Zhang-Yang-Wei on the redundancy of D-semifaithful codes `[V]` for the mentions; the papers themselves were not read.
- Compound and universal lossy coding with the distortion measure itself varying: Merhav, "D-semifaithful codes that are universal over both memoryless sources and distortion measures", arXiv:2203.03305 `[V-abs]` (redundancy `O(log n / n)` above the empirical rate-distortion function of every source vector, for every bounded additive distortion); Mahmood and Wagner, "Lossy compression with universal distortion", arXiv:2110.07022, and "Minimax rate-distortion", arXiv:2202.04481 `[V-abs]` (distortion measure and level revealed only at run time).

**Are negative ("credit") distortions covered?** Not literally: the lemma is stated for a nonnegative function (Csiszar 1998; Palaiyanur et al.; Kostina-Verdu take `d : A x B -> [0, +inf]`). They are covered after the standard normalisation. Subtract from `rho_1(a,y) = c 1[a != y] - e(y)` its minimum over `a`, which is `-e(y)` and depends on the covered letter only. The constraint `rho(a,y) <= 0` becomes `c d_H(a,y) <= sum_i e(y_i) = n E_P e` for `y` of type `P`, that is, Hamming covering of each type class at the type-dependent level `D_P = E_P[e]/c`. Then `max_P N_n(P, D_P) <= min|A| <= (n+1)^{|Sigma|} max_P N_n(P, D_P)`, and `R_P` is the Hamming rate-distortion function of `P` at level `D_P`. The lemma as printed is uniform in `P` for a fixed level; uniformity in the level follows from monotonicity of `N_n(P, .)` and joint continuity of `R(P, D)` on the compact parameter set, or from the quantitative versions. This requires `e >= 0`; a type with negative mean credit cannot be covered at all. Polyanskiy-Wu's general converse is stated for real-valued distortions `[V]`, so the converse half needs no normalisation.

**Verdict.** KNOWN. Textbook (type covering lemma and its stated consequence `max_P R(P,D)`); the credit and type-dependent-level version is a routine consequence.

**Confidence.** High.

**How the claim would have to be sharpened to be new.**
1. *Sites that are not identical.* With site-dependent `c_i` and `e_i` there are no types. The exponent should then be given by a variational formula over tilting parameters, with T7 as the converse and a random or greedy cover as the achievability. A clean statement for independent, non-identical sites, and for tree-structured credits, was not found (not searched specifically).
2. *Finite `n`.* Explicit polynomial factors in the style of the redundancy literature, so that the statement gives certified numbers for chain lengths of interest.
3. *Connect to T6.* T8 is a zero-error statement (every `y` is covered), hence a maximum over types. The certificate problem of T6 needs coverage only on average under the Gibbs measure with an exponential weight. The gap between `R_pi` and `max_P R_P` is the quantity to analyse.
