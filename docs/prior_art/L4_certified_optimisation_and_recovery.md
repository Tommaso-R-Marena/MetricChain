# L4 prior-art check: certified optimisation under an envelope, certificate complexity, optimal recovery, certified partition functions

Literature check performed on 2026-11-24 for the MetricChain program. Setting common to all claims: `X` is a finite set (or a metric space); `d` is an envelope with `d(x,x) = 0` and the triangle inequality, possibly asymmetric (a quasi-metric); an unknown residual `g` satisfies `g(x) - g(y) <= d(x,y)` and can be evaluated exactly at chosen points (anchors); `f` is fully known; the energy is `E = f + g`. The goal is to certify an `eps`-optimal point of `E`, or bounds on `sum_x exp(-beta E(x))`, for every `g` consistent with the envelope and the evaluations. With `f = 0` this is certified Lipschitz global optimisation. Covers candidate claims C1-C9 of this brief. The numbering is local to this report; the sibling reports L1-L3 use their own C-numbers.

## Reading guide

- **Verdicts.** `KNOWN` (a citation states it), `KNOWN-IN-SPIRIT` (an immediate corollary or routine combination of cited results, or stated in a neighbouring field), `PARTIALLY KNOWN` (special cases or weaker forms are published; the stated form was not found), `NO MATCH FOUND` (the listed searches did not surface it; this is not a proof of novelty).
- **Evidence tags.** `[V]` = the quoted statement, equation or theorem was read in the paper's own text in this session (publisher, arXiv, author copy). `[V-abs]` = verified from the abstract only, or from the text of another paper that states the result (the entry then says "via ..."). `[U]` = not verified in this session; the bibliographic record may still be confirmed, and the entry says so.
- **Bibliographic records.** Journal records (authors, title, volume, pages, year, DOI) were checked against Crossref unless an entry says otherwise. Conference papers without Crossref records (PMLR, NeurIPS) were checked on arXiv or on the proceedings pages.
- **How texts were read.** PDFs were downloaded and text-extracted, and the passages read directly. Relation symbols and stars are sometimes lost in extraction; where that happened the entry says so and the symbol is restored from the proof. A few web pages were read through an automated fetch-and-summarise tool; those entries are limited to `[V-abs]`.
- **Reviewer's derivations.** Short arguments marked as such are mine. They show why a claim is or is not a corollary of the cited literature and have not been refereed. The one for C7 was sanity-checked by brute force on small random instances with throwaway scripts that are not part of the repository.
- **Conventions.** The literature mostly maximises a function `f` that is `L`-Lipschitz. This brief minimises `E = f + g` under an envelope `d`. Statements are translated without comment; `gap(y)` is the optimality gap of `y`.
- **Baseline.** Each claim is compared with the strongest work found, including 2024-2026 preprints, not only with the papers named in the brief.
- **Relation to sibling reports.** L1 (item C2) covers envelope-based dominance pruning and iMinDEE. L2 (items C8(d), C8(e)) covers worst-case complexity of log-partition estimation and competitive refinement. L5 (item P4, as found in the working tree today) covers the abstract-convexity reading of Lipschitz envelopes and of the Piyavskii-Shubert algorithm as a generalised cutting-plane method. They are referred to, not repeated.
- **What the claims were checked against.** The statements in the brief, and where present the formal statements in the working tree (`proofs/Frontiers/CertificationGeometry.lean`, `CertificateComplexity.lean`, `BestFirst.lean`, untracked on 2026-11-24). No file other than this report was changed.

## C1. Slack reformulation: a certificate is a gap-radius covering

**Claim as checked.** With the slack `s(a,y) = d(a,y) - g(a) + g(y) >= 0`, after evaluating `g` on `A` the best certified lower bound on `min E` is `min_y [E(y) - s(A,y)]` and the certified upper bound is `min_A E`. So `A` certifies `eps`-optimality iff every `y` is within slack distance `gap(y) + eps` of `A`.

**Reviewer's derivation (the claim is correct, and the two forms of the covering are the same statement).** The lower McShane envelope `L_A(y) = max_{a in A} [g(a) - d(a,y)]` satisfies the envelope constraint and agrees with `g` on `A`, also for asymmetric `d` (the extension theorem for quasi-metrics is item 13). Hence `f + L_A = E - s(A, .)` is the pointwise tightest lower bound. With `U = min_A E`, anchor `a` certifies `y` iff `s(a,y) <= E(y) - U + eps`. Subtracting `g(y)` from both sides gives the equivalent form `d(a,y) + f(a) - f(y) <= E(a) - U + eps`. For `f = 0` this reads: *the evaluation at `a` certifies the `d`-ball of radius `gap(a) + eps` around `a`*. That is the classical picture of cones under a sawtooth bound. Radii attached to anchors in the metric `d` and radii attached to the covered points in the slack quasi-metric are two readings of one inequality. One caveat for `f != 0`: the tightest upper bound on the optimal *value* over the version space is `min_y [f(y) + min_a (g(a) + d(y,a))]`, which can be smaller than `min_A E`. This does not affect certification of the incumbent. It matters if the claim is read as a statement about the value interval, the distinction between `Opt` and `Opt*` in K8 and between `n_P` and `n_PY` in K3.

**Closest prior art and the instance-dependent quantities it uses.**

1. K3 (Hansen, Jaumard, Lu 1991). `[V-abs]` The benchmark `n_B` is the number of evaluations of "a best possible algorithm", that is, the smallest certificate, in dimension one. Via K1: the upper bound for Piyavskii's algorithm is proportional to `int_0^1 dx / (f(x*) - f(x) + eps)`. The bounds on `n_B` already depend on the ratio between the true constant `L_0` and the constant `L_1` used.
2. K4 (Danilin 1971). `[U]` First comparison with a best possible algorithm, via K5 and K7.
3. K5 and K6 (Baran, Demaine, Katz). `[V]` The name is "proof set": a set `P` such that every admissible function equal to the true one on `P` has the same correct output. "OPT is equal to the size of the smallest proof set." This is the certificate of the claim, defined for a general black-box Lipschitz problem.
4. K1 (Bachoc, Cesari, Gerchinovitz 2021). `[V]` The same object is `tau` (Eq. 7), the stopping time of the best certified algorithm on `f`. The instance-dependent quantity is `S_C(f, eps)`, a sum of packing numbers of gap layers at the scale of the gap, equivalent to `int_X dx / (f(x*) - f(x) + eps)^d`. The lower bound is `sigma(A, f, eps) > c'_d (1 - Lip(f)/L)^d / (1 + m_eps) * S_C(f, eps)` for `f` with `Lip(f) < L` strictly, where `L` is the constant known to the algorithm and the certificate must be valid for every `L`-Lipschitz function. For `d = 1` strictness is not needed (Proposition 2). For `d >= 2` the bound fails at `Lip(f) = L` (Proposition 3).
5. K2 (Bouttier, Cesari, Ducoffe, Gerchinovitz 2020). `[V]` Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4 are the covering statement read from the algorithm's side: a query with gap `Delta` excludes all later queries from its `Delta/L`-ball, and before certification all queries are `eps/L`-separated.

## C2. Grey-box version: known arbitrary part plus Lipschitz residual

**Claim as checked.** The theory of C1 with a known, arbitrary (non-Lipschitz) `f`; the relevant cost is the tilted quasi-metric `c(a,y) = d(a,y) + f(a) - f(y)`.

**Reviewer's remark on what changes.** In the black-box case the gap is itself Lipschitz, so for `kappa < 1` all clients of an anchor have comparable radii. ## C4. Partition-function lower bound

**Claim as checked.** For every `t >= 0` and every `eps`-certificate `A`, `|A| >= e^{-t eps} sum_y e^{-t gap(y)} / max_a sum_y e^{-t s(a,y)}`. For a product space with weighted-Hamming envelope and `kappa`-Lipschitz `g` the denominator is at most `prod_i (1 + (k_i - 1) e^{-t (1-kappa) c_i})`.

**Reviewer's derivation: three readings of the same inequality.**
- *Direct.* Each `y` has an anchor with `s(a,y) <= gap(y) + eps`, so `1 <= sum_{a in A} e^{t (gap(y) + eps - s(a,y))}`. Multiply by `e^{-t gap(y)}` and sum over `y`.
- *As a restricted dual of C3.* Take the fooling measure proportional to `e^{-t gap}` and relax the indicator of coverage by `1[s <= gap + eps] <= e^{t (gap + eps - s)}`. So the bound never exceeds `N_frac`. The volume form `sum_y 1/M_y <= |A|` (`card_ge_sum_inv` in the working tree) is another dual solution, with weights `1/M_y`; the integral of K1 is its continuum version.
- *As a rate-distortion bound.* Let `pi_t` be the Gibbs measure proportional to `e^{-t gap}` and `rho(y,a) = s(a,y) - gap(y)` the excess slack. A certificate is a quantizer with `rho <= eps` everywhere, so `log |A| >= R_{pi_t, rho}(eps)`. The dual characterisation of `R(D)` (item 1) with the constant test function gives `R(eps) >= -t eps - log max_a sum_y pi_t(y) e^{-t rho(y,a)}`, which is the claim. Optimising the test function gives the full `R(eps)`, which is at least as large.

**Reviewer's comparison with the integral of K1.** For `f = 0`, `X` in `R^d`, norm envelope with constant `L`: `Z(t) := int_X e^{-t gap(y)} dy` and `int e^{-t (1-kappa) L ||y||} dy = d! v_1 / (t (1-kappa) L)^d`, so the claim reads `N >= c_d ((1-kappa) L)^d sup_t t^d e^{-t eps} Z(t)`. By `int_0^infty t^{d-1} e^{-t u} dt = (d-1)! / u^d`, the integral of K1 is `int_X dy / (gap(y) + eps)^d = (1/(d-1)!) int_0^infty t^d e^{-t eps} Z(t) dt/t`. The claim is a supremum over scales `t`; the integral of K1 is the integral over scales against `dt/t`. The two differ by at most the logarithmic number of relevant scales. This is the `1/(1 + m_eps)` between the lower and upper bounds of K1 (maximum against sum over layers). In `R^d` the claim therefore has the structure of K1, Theorem 2: the same factor `(1-kappa)^d` and the loss of one logarithm against the integral, possibly with better constants. Its additional content is in spaces without polynomial volume growth.

## C5. Phase transition for product landscapes

**Claim as checked.** For `f(x) = sum_i phi_i(x_i)`, zero true residual and envelope constants `c_i`: one evaluation certifies the optimum iff every single-site excitation energy is at least `c_i`; otherwise the certificate size is exponential in the number of sites, with a rate given by a rate-distortion (Legendre) formula.

**Reviewer's check of the first half.** With `g = 0`, `{a}` is a 0-certificate iff `d(a,y) <= E(y) - E(a)` for all `y`, that is `sum_i c_i 1[y_i != a_i] <= sum_i (phi_i(y_i) - phi_i(a_i))`. Single-site deviations give necessity and additivity gives sufficiency. This is the envelope dominance criterion of L1 (item C2(b)) applied at every site. It is also the grey-box counterpart of the boundary phenomenon in K1 and K7: there a single evaluation can certify a large region only when the function has slope exactly `L`; here the known part may be steeper than the envelope.

**Closest prior art.**

1. Black-box Lipschitz optimisation on Hamming-type spaces. C. Malherbe, A. Grosnit, R. Tutunov, H. Bou Ammar, J. Wang, "Optimistic tree searches for combinatorial black-box optimization", NeurIPS 2022 ([record](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/nips/MalherbeGTB022.html)) (Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 35, pp. 33080-33092) `[V-abs]`: a tree search (OLTS) that "assumes the Lipschitz constant of the function to be known" and a variant for unknown constant. A search-engine excerpt of the paper shows Lipschitz classes with respect to the Hamming distance on `{0,1}^d`. No certificates, no known part.
2. The rate formula as a tool. Type covering lemma: T. Berger, "Rate Distortion Theory", Prentice-Hall, 1971, and I. Csiszár, J. Körner, "Information Theory: Coding Theorems for Discrete Memoryless Systems", chapter "A covering lemma and the error exponent in source coding". `[V-abs]` via secondary sources found by search (attribution of the lemma to Berger and its presence in Csiszár-Körner); statements not read. Parametric (Legendre) form of `R(D)`: C4 item 1 `[V]`.
3. Worst case on product domains. For a constant function the certificate is a covering code, so the count is at least the sphere-covering bound (C4 item 4). Instance-dependent statements for separable landscapes were not found.
4. Verification complexity that a referee will raise. M. Frances, A. Litman, "On covering problems of codes", Theory Comput. Syst. 30(2):113-119 (1997), [doi:10.1007/BF02679443](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02679443) `[V-abs]` (search excerpt of the abstract): "the decision problem associated with the Covering Radius of arbitrary binary codes is NP-complete". V. Guruswami, D. Micciancio, O. Regev, "The complexity of the covering radius problem", Comput. Complex. 14(2):90-121 (2005), [doi:10.1007/s00037-005-0193-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00037-005-0193-y) `[V-abs]`: for codes, NP-hard for every constant approximation factor and `Pi_2`-hard for some. Reviewer's inference: when `X` is a product space given succinctly, even checking that a listed anchor set is a certificate is co-NP-hard in general, since with `f = g = 0` it is a covering-radius question.

**What was not found.** The dichotomy "one evaluation or exponentially many"; an explicit exponent for certificate size on separable landscapes; any treatment of a known separable part plus Hamming-Lipschitz residual. Queries combining Hamming or product spaces, bounded differences, separable objectives and certificates returned only K1, K2 and item 1.

**Verdict.** NO MATCH FOUND. A referee would see the first half as immediate and the second as an application of the type covering lemma, and would judge it on the explicitness and tightness of the rate. **Confidence:** medium.

**How to sharpen it into something new.**
1. Prove both directions with explicit finite-size terms, for heterogeneous sites, and for a true residual that is `kappa`-Lipschitz rather than zero. State the exponent as a convex program and show continuity or a jump at the threshold.
2. Go beyond product landscapes: nearest-neighbour chains via transfer matrices, where the rate becomes a spectral radius. This is where the application sits.
3. Address item 4: identify the families of anchor sets whose validity can be checked in polynomial time on a product space, since certificates that cannot be checked are of limited use.

## C6. Hardness of computing a minimum certificate

**Claim as checked.** Computing a minimum certificate is NP-hard and as hard to approximate as set cover (reduction from dominating set).

**Reviewer's derivation.** With `f = 0`, `g = 0`, `d` the shortest-path metric of a graph and `eps = 1`, a set is a certificate iff it is a dominating set. (The working tree uses an exact-certificate variant with one extra point and a 0/1-valued known part, giving domination number plus one.) So the problem contains minimum dominating set for explicitly listed `X`, and membership in NP is clear. With arbitrary `f` and an asymmetric envelope it contains asymmetric priority k-center.

**Closest prior art.**

1. k-center and its priority version: K10 `[V]` via Bajpai et al. (NP-hardness of approximating k-center within `2 - eps` by Hsu-Nemhauser; factor 2 with the optimal number of centres by Hochbaum-Shmoys and, for client radii, Plesník).
2. Asymmetric versions: K11 `[V]` (no polynomial-time computable approximation factor for asymmetric priority k-center unless P = NP).
3. Set cover thresholds: U. Feige, "A threshold of ln n for approximating set cover", J. ACM 45(4):634-652 (1998), [doi:10.1145/285055.285059](https://doi.org/10.1145/285055.285059); I. Dinur, D. Steurer, "Analytical approach to parallel repetition", STOC 2014, pp. 624-633, [doi:10.1145/2591796.2591884](https://doi.org/10.1145/2591796.2591884). `[U]` (records confirmed; the statements, and the standard equivalence of dominating set and set cover under approximation-preserving reductions, are recalled).
4. The same result for other certificate notions. Goldman-Kearns and Shinohara-Miyano (C3 item 3) `[V]`: optimal teaching sequences are NP-hard and equivalent to set cover. Explorable uncertainty: G. Charalambous, M. Hoffmann, "Verification problem of maximal points under uncertainty", IWOCA 2013, LNCS 8288, pp. 94-105, [doi:10.1007/978-3-642-45278-9_9](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-45278-9_9) `[V-abs]` (via a later paper's summary: the verification problem is NP-hard); T. Erlebach, M. Hoffmann, F. Kammer, "Query-competitive algorithms for cheapest set problems under uncertainty", Theor. Comput. Sci. 613:51-64 (2016), [doi:10.1016/j.tcs.2015.11.025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2015.11.025) `[U]` (record confirmed). See also L2, item C8(e).
5. Lipschitz optimisation itself. K1 and K2 discuss the cost of the inner maximisation of the Piyavskii-Shubert algorithm, "believed to grow as `n^Omega(d)`", not the complexity of optimal certificates. No hardness result for optimal sampling sets of certified Lipschitz optimisation was found.

**Verdict.** KNOWN-IN-SPIRIT. A referee would call it immediate from dominating set and would not count it as a contribution without more. **Confidence:** high.

**How to sharpen it into something new.**
1. A classification instead of a single hardness statement: minimum certificates are (i) set-cover hard to approximate in count at fixed radii, (ii) polynomially solvable with the optimal count if radii may be relaxed by a constant, for symmetric envelopes and explicitly listed `X` (Plesník), (iii) inapproximable for asymmetric envelopes (Gørtz-Wirth). Items (i) to (iii) are corollaries once C1 is in place, and together they are worth stating.
2. Hardness for the structured instances that matter: product spaces with additive or chain-structured `f`, where the trivial reduction does not apply and even verification is hard (C5 item 4).

## C7. Resource-augmentation instance-optimality of best-first search

**Claim as checked.** In any metric space and for any known `f`, if `g` is `kappa`-Lipschitz with `kappa < 1`, the Piyavskii-Shubert (best-first) algorithm stops after at most `N*(theta-shrunk envelope, eps/C) + 1` evaluations, with no doubling or dimension assumption. The comparator is the smallest anchor set that covers every `y` within slack radius `theta (gap(y) + eps/C)`. This is the form stated in the working tree (`proofs/Frontiers/BestFirst.lean`, `bestFirst_card_le`): symmetric envelope, `theta = (1-kappa)/(4(1+kappa))`, tolerance `eps/6`, bound `|C| + 1`. Rescaling `d` for the comparator by `1/theta`, which makes certification harder, is equivalent up to constants depending on `kappa`.

**What is known.**

1. Dimension one, same envelope, constant factor. K3 `[V-abs]`: `n_P <= 2 n_B + 1` for bounding the value and `n_PY <= 4 n_B + 1` for value and point, both sharp. No slack between the true and the assumed Lipschitz constant is needed (consistent with K1, Proposition 2).
2. Impossibility without slack in dimension two and higher. K7 `[V]`, Theorems 11 and 12: `OPT = 2` against `Omega(eps^(1-d))` samples on the ball; `OPT = O(1)` against `Omega(sqrt(1/eps))` on the square. K1 `[V]`, Proposition 3, shows the same source of trouble from the other side: at `Lip(f) = L` a certificate of size 2 exists while `S_C` is polynomially large in `1/eps`. All these instances have `Lip = L`, that is `kappa = 1`.
3. Slack in the Lipschitz constant restores near-optimality in `R^d`. K1 `[V]`, Theorems 2 and 3, with K2, Theorem 3.6: for `Lip(f) < L`, the complexity of c.DOO and of Piyavskii-Shubert is within a factor of order `C_d (1 - Lip(f)/L)^-d (1 + log2(eps_0/eps))` of that of any certified algorithm. This is resource augmentation in substance: the comparator is handicapped by a larger constant than the truth. It is stated through packing numbers and an integral, with a logarithmic factor and constants exponential in `d`.
4. A logarithmic factor can be unavoidable for related problems. K5 `[V]`: `O(OPT log(eps^-1/OPT))` samples for the nearest point on a Lipschitz curve, with a matching lower bound on some instances. K6 `[V]`: the same for deterministic integration. K6 also compares with the optimum at tolerance `eps/2`, via `DOPT(f, eps/2) <= 2 DOPT(f, eps) + 1`. Tolerance augmentation is thus an established device in this literature.
5. General metric spaces. Worst case: the covering constant, with no gain from adaption (K8, Proposition 1.3.6; see C8). Instance-dependent upper bounds: near-optimality dimension (C1 item 7) and zooming dimension (C1 item 8), for symmetric dissimilarities and without certificates. K1 states that its lower bound and integral characterisation "exploit the normed space structure".
6. The offline analogue of the claim. K10 `[V]`: for client radii `r_v`, a greedy packing ordered by radius has at most as many points as any feasible solution and serves every client within `2 r_v`. This is a resource-augmentation guarantee with factor 1 in cardinality and 2 in radius. K11 `[V]`: no such guarantee is possible in polynomial time for asymmetric distances.
7. Resource augmentation and instance optimality as concepts. T. Roughgarden, "Resource augmentation", Chapter 4 of "Beyond the Worst-Case Analysis of Algorithms", Cambridge University Press 2021, pp. 72-92, [doi:10.1017/9781108637435.006](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108637435.006), [arXiv:2007.13234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13234) `[V-abs]`. Origins: D. D. Sleator, R. E. Tarjan, Commun. ACM 28(2):202-208 (1985), [doi:10.1145/2786.2793](https://doi.org/10.1145/2786.2793); B. Kalyanasundaram, K. Pruhs, J. ACM 47(4):617-643 (2000), [doi:10.1145/347476.347479](https://doi.org/10.1145/347476.347479). `[U]` (records confirmed.) In adaptive approximation, near-best guarantees of the form "error with `N` degrees of freedom at most `C_1` times the best error with `c_2 N`" are standard: P. Binev, R. DeVore, "Fast computation in adaptive tree approximation", Numer. Math. 97(2):193-217 (2004), [doi:10.1007/s00211-003-0493-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00211-003-0493-6) `[V-abs]` (the displayed form is confirmed for Binev's later hp-adaptive paper through a search excerpt, not for the 2004 paper).

**Reviewer's derivation (why the claim is a routine adaptation).** Written before I looked at the repository's proof; it arrives at the same constants. Let `d` be symmetric, `s` the slack, `rho := theta (1+kappa)/(1-kappa)`. Let `x_1, x_2, ...` be the queries, and let `B` cover every `y` within `s(B,y) <= theta (gap(y) + eps')`. Suppose `x_i, x_j` with `2 <= i < j` are served by the same `b`. From `(1-kappa) d <= s <= (1+kappa) d` and the triangle inequality, `s(x_i,x_j)` and `s(x_j,x_i)` are at most `rho (gap(x_i) + gap(x_j) + 2 eps')`. Because `x_i` minimised the lower bound when it was chosen, `gap(x_i) - gap(x_j) <= s(x_j, x_i)`; this holds for arbitrary `f`. Because `x_j` was queried after `x_i` and before stopping, `s(x_i,x_j) >= gap(x_j)` and `s(x_i,x_j) > gap(x_j) - gap(x_i) + eps` (K2, Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4, in slack form). For `rho < 1/3` these inequalities force `eps < C eps'`. With `rho = 1/4`, that is `theta = (1-kappa)/(4(1+kappa))`, one gets `C = 6`. Hence the queries after the first are served by distinct points of `B`, and the number of evaluations is at most `N*_theta(eps/6) + 1`. The `+1` pays for the arbitrary first query. The argument is the packing-against-cover step of Hochbaum-Shmoys and Plesník, with the selection rule of the algorithm supplying the ordering by radius. Sanity check: on 26,219 runs over random instances with 4 to 9 points (planar, graph and star metrics; `kappa` in {0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9}; random `f`) there was no violation and the bound was attained with equality 39 times. Without augmentation the algorithm exceeded the true minimal certificate plus one in 1,966 of 19,343 runs, by a factor of up to 6.

**Reviewer's remarks.**
- Radius augmentation cannot be dropped in general metric spaces, even for `kappa = 0`. Star metric with `n` leaves at distance 1 from the hub and 2 from each other, `f = g = 0`, `eps = 1`: the hub alone is a certificate, and the algorithm started at a leaf evaluates all `n` leaves.
- The symmetric envelope is used twice. With an asymmetric envelope the slack is no longer comparable in the two directions, and K11 suggests that no constant-factor statement can hold without an asymmetry parameter.
- In spaces of exponential growth (Hamming-type products) shrinking radii by a constant changes the exponent of `N*`. The statement is then only as informative as the stability of `N*` under rescaling, which the rate functions of C4 and C5 would have to quantify.

**What was not found.** A comparison of the Piyavskii-Shubert algorithm, DOO or zooming with the exact minimal certificate of a harder instance (shrunk radii, tighter tolerance); the phrase "resource augmentation" in connection with Lipschitz optimisation; any dimension-free instance-optimality statement in a metric space.

**Verdict.** PARTIALLY KNOWN. The phenomenon is established: exact in one dimension (K3), impossible at `kappa = 1` in higher dimension (K7), near-optimal with slack in `R^d` up to a logarithm (K1). The dimension-free resource-augmentation formulation was not found and is a real sharpening of the form of the result. A referee would call the proof a routine adaptation of Plesník's 2-approximation for priority k-center and of Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4 of Bouttier et al., and would insist on K7 and K10 being cited. **Confidence:** medium. The approximation-algorithms and adaptive-analysis literatures were sampled, not exhausted.

**How to sharpen it into something new.**
1. Convert augmentation into a constant factor where possible. For `f = 0` in a metric space with doubling constant `lambda`, the clients of an anchor `b` form the ball of radius `gap(b) + eps` and all have gap at least `(1-kappa)(gap(b) + eps) - eps`. A shrunk cover then costs a factor `lambda^{O(log((1+kappa)/(1-kappa)))}`. This would give "evaluations `<= K(lambda, kappa) N* + 1`" with no `log(1/eps)`: the higher-dimensional analogue of `n_PY <= 4 n_B + 1`, and an instance-optimality statement that K1 proves only up to `1 + m_eps`. I did not find it in K1 or K2 and have only sketched it; if it holds it is the most publishable consequence.
2. Prove necessity: of the `+1`; of `theta(kappa) -> 0` as `kappa -> 1` (K7, Theorem 11, is the natural construction); of radius augmentation outside doubling spaces (the star example, or a version that defeats every algorithm).
3. Decide the grey-box case. With arbitrary `f` the conversion in point 1 fails as sketched, because the clients of one anchor have unrelated radii. Either a logarithmic loss is necessary there, as in K5 and K6, or a different argument is needed.

## C8. Optimal recovery and information-based complexity

**Claim as checked.** For the Lipschitz class with function values, the central algorithm is the midpoint of the McShane envelopes; adaption does not help in the worst case for global optimisation over Lipschitz classes. ## C9. Finite-temperature analogue: certified partition functions

**Claim as checked.** For certified bounds on `Z = sum_x exp(-beta E(x))` the certified ratio is `Z_hi / Z = E_pi exp(beta s(A, Y))` under the Gibbs measure `pi`; hence the anchor set is a quantizer of `pi`, and `|A| >= exp R(eps/beta)` with `R` the rate-distortion function of `pi` for the slack distortion.

**Reviewer's derivation.** `Z_hi = sum_y exp(-beta (E(y) - s(A,y)))` gives the identity. If `log(Z_hi/Z) <= eps`, Jensen gives `E_pi s(A,Y) <= eps/beta`, so `A` is a quantizer of `pi` with mean slack distortion at most `eps/beta`, and `log |A| >= I(Y; q(Y)) >= R(eps/beta)`. The identity is elementary and the bound is the converse of rate-distortion theory. Jensen discards the exponential moment, which is dominated by rare points of large slack, so the true exponent is governed by a different variational problem.

**Closest prior art.**

1. Instance-optimal certified integration of Lipschitz functions. K6 `[V]`: `O(DOPT log(eps^-1/DOPT))` deterministic samples, which cannot be improved in general; `O(ROPT^(4/3) + ROPT log(1/eps))` samples with randomisation, also unimprovable in general; "DOPT is equal to the size of a smallest proof set". The uncertainty is measured by the "area looseness" between the two Lipschitz envelopes. Their example: for `f(x) = L x` two evaluations suffice at every tolerance. This is the integration counterpart of K3, in one dimension, for a linear functional. **How to sharpen it into something new.**
1. Replace Jensen by the exact constraint. The certificate requires `E_pi exp(beta s(A,Y)) <= e^eps`, an exponential-moment (risk-sensitive) distortion constraint. Compute the exact exponent for product landscapes and show how it differs from `R(eps/beta)`. That difference is the content.
2. Prove a matching upper bound, by greedy covering or random coding, in the product setting, with the low-temperature limit recovering C4 and C5 and the high-temperature limit recovering plain quantization of the uniform measure.
3. State the result against K6: what replaces the necessary `log(eps^-1/OPT)` factor of one-dimensional integration when the functional is `log Z` and the space is a product space.

## 10. Where novelty most plausibly sits

- Not in C1, C3, C6 or C8 as stated. C1 and C3 are a reformulation and a routine LP; C6 is immediate; C8 is classical. Their value is as a framework that joins three literatures (global optimisation, adaptive analysis of algorithms, bandit optimisation) which do not fully cite each other. Citing K5 to K7, which the recent bandit papers miss, is necessary in any case: they contain the notion of certificate, the impossibility result behind the hypothesis `kappa < 1`, and the integration baseline for C9.
- In C7, especially if the resource-augmentation statement can be converted into constant-factor instance-optimality in doubling spaces without the logarithm of K1, and if necessity of each form of augmentation is shown.
- In C2, as the theorem that the grey-box problem is priority covering with arbitrary client radii, with a quantified value of the white-box part.
- In C4, C5 and C9 together, as a thermodynamic description of certificate complexity on product spaces: exponents, matching upper bounds, and the exact risk-sensitive form at finite temperature. The inequalities alone are standard duality.
- A point a referee will press throughout: on succinctly described product spaces, verifying a certificate is itself hard in general (C5 item 4).
