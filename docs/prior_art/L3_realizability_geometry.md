# L3 prior-art check: 3D realizability, distance/superposition stability, cycle holonomy, torsion-to-RMSD propagation, expressivity and sampling

Literature check performed on 2026-11-24 for the MetricChain program (chain molecules: `L` points or frames in `R^3` produced by an articulated torsion-angle chain; predicted squared-distance matrices, frame-error matrices and noisy relative `SE(3)` transforms). It covers candidate claims C10 to C14.

Notation used throughout: `Dhat` is a hollow symmetric matrix of predicted squared distances; `J = I - 11^T/L`; `G = -(1/2) J Dhat J` with eigenvalues `lambda_1 >= ... >= lambda_{L-1}` on the centered subspace; `K(E)_ij = E_ii + E_jj - 2 E_ij`; `Delta(X) = K(X X^T)` is the squared-distance matrix of a configuration `X in R^{L x r}`; `tau = sum_{k>r} lambda_k` is the tail trace; "s-stress" is `||Dhat - Delta(X)||_F^2` and "strain" is `||G - X X^T||_F^2`.

## C10. Spectral lower bound on the irreducible realization error (s-stress)

**Claim as checked.** (a) For symmetric `E` with `E1 = 0`: `||K(E)||_F^2 = 4||E||_F^2 + 2L||diag(E)||^2 + 2(tr E)^2`. (b) `min_{X in R^{L x r}} ||Dhat - Delta(X)||_F^2 >= 4 sum_{k>r} lambda_k^2 + (4/(r+1)) tau^2`, improving the strain bound by a factor of about `sqrt(L)` for uniformly biased distances, "with equality for vertex-transitive (e.g. circulant) inputs". (c) `2 dist(G, PSD_{rank<=r}) <= min_X ||Dhat - Delta(X)||_F <= 2 sqrt(1+L) dist(G, PSD_{rank<=r})`.

**Reviewer's derivation (the inequalities are correct; the equality statement is not).** Since `Dhat = K(G)` for hollow `Dhat` and `Delta(X) = K(X X^T)` for centered `X`, the residual is `K(E)` with `E = G - X X^T`. Expanding the square and using `E1 = 0` gives (a). Cauchy-Schwarz on the diagonal, `||diag E||^2 >= (tr E)^2 / L`, gives `||K(E)||^2 >= 4||E||^2 + 4(tr E)^2`. By Hoffman-Wielandt the right-hand side is minimised over rank-`r` PSD matrices by one commuting with `G`; minimising `sum_{k<=r} d_k^2 + (sum_{k<=r} d_k + tau)^2` over the eigenvalue shifts `d_k` gives `d_k = -tau/(r+1)` and the value `tau^2/(r+1)`. This is (b). Equality needs the relaxed optimiser `B* = sum_{k<=r} (lambda_k + tau/(r+1)) v_k v_k^T` to be PSD and `diag(G - B*)` to be constant.

Its proof relaxes the sign constraints and subtracts `C2/(r+1)` from the `r` kept eigenvalues and from the trace slot, which is the computation above. Combined with the relaxation inequality: `min_X s-stress >= 4 sum_{k>r} lambda_k^2 + 4 tau^2/(r+1)`. **This is C10(b) with identical constants.**
   - Their Theorem 3 and Algorithm 2 compute `D_l` exactly in `O(n^3)`, keeping the sign constraints, so `||D_l - D||_F^2` is a computable lower bound that is at least as strong as the closed form. Their Figure 2 reports that this lower bound "tracks the true SSTRESS solution extremely closely" on the tested data.

**Verdict.**

- (a) KNOWN (items 2 and 1).
- (b) KNOWN, including the constants `4` and `4/(r+1)` (items 1 and 3). The observation that the trace term is what the additive-constant obstruction looks like spectrally is also in items 1-3 ("This term is from the excess in the trace", Sonthalia et al.). The stated gain for uniformly biased distances is correct: adding `c` to all off-diagonal entries shifts every eigenvalue of `G` by `c/2`, so the bound grows from about `c^2 L` to about `c^2 L^2/(r+1)`, a factor `sqrt(L/(r+1))` in norm. The regular simplex, which is the pure-bias case, is the tightness example of item 2.
- (c) KNOWN-IN-SPIRIT / FOLKLORE (items 2, 5, 6). The stated upper constant `2 sqrt(1+L)` can be replaced by `2 sqrt(L)`, which is the operator norm of `K` on centered matrices and is attained at `E` proportional to `J`.
- **Equality case: repair needed.** "Equality for vertex-transitive (e.g. circulant) inputs" is false as stated. Counterexamples found numerically: (i) the regular simplex `Dhat = 11^T - I`, for which the closed form equals `L(L-1-r)/(r+1)`, that is `(L-1-r)/((r+1)(L-1))` after dividing by `||Dhat||_F^2`. With `L = 5`, `r = 3`: bound `1.25`, minimum s-stress `1.3043` (about `30/23`); for `r = 3` the bound is attained for `L = 4` and `L = 6, ..., 9`. This matches the existence pattern of spherical 2-designs on `S^2` (4 points or at least 6), because equality for the simplex needs `L` points on a sphere in `R^r` with zero centroid and isotropic second moment `[existence pattern unverified-from-memory; Mimura 1990]`. (ii) Circulant inputs with `r = 3` (elementwise powers of the regular 9-gon and 12-gon squared distances): the cut at `r = 3` splits a degenerate cosine/sine eigen-pair and the bound is strict (for example `5.742` against `5.908`), while `r = 2` and `r = 4` give equality to `1e-14`. A correct sufficient condition is: the top-`r` spectral subspace is a union of full eigenspaces that are invariant under a vertex-transitive automorphism group, and `lambda_r + tau/(r+1) >= 0`.

**Confidence.** High. The two key sources were read in full text and their formulas were matched term by term to the claim.

**How the claim could be sharpened to be new.**

1. *Close the gap that items 1-3 leave open.* By identity (a) the exact problem is `min_B [4||E||^2 + 4(tr E)^2 + 2L ||diag E - (tr E / L) 1||^2]`, and every bound above discards the last term (Sonthalia's `C3`). A lower bound that keeps a provable share of the diagonal-variance term, for example through a dual certificate or through an eigenvector-delocalisation quantity such as `||(U∘U) lambda||`, together with a correct and complete characterisation of equality (the 2-design condition above), would go beyond the located literature. Chain inputs are far from vertex-transitive (chain ends), so this term should matter there.
2. *Bounds for the data that structure predictors emit.* None of the located results handles weights or missing entries (distogram confidences, distance cut-offs), unsquared distances, or hard chain constraints (fixed bond lengths as a band of `Dhat`). A certificate of the form "every `X in R^{L x 3}` has weighted distance error at least `c`", stated in Ångström and valid under known bond constraints, was not found.

## C11. Distances versus superposition RMSD

**Claim as checked.** (a) `dRMSD <= sqrt(2) RMSD`: who states the sharp constant? (b) `min_g ||X - gY||_F^2 <= ||Delta(X) - Delta(Y)||_F^2 / (8(sqrt2 - 1) sigma_r(X)^2)`, minimum over rigid motions including reflections, with `sigma_r(X)^2 = L` times the smallest eigenvalue of the gyration tensor; applied to molecular comparison? sharp? (c) `TM-score >= max_i (1/L) sum_j 1/(1 + (P_ij/d0)^2)` with `P` the true frame-aligned point error; rigorous local-to-global guarantees from lDDT or PAE to RMSD.

### C11(a)

**Reviewer's derivation.** After optimal superposition let `delta_i = x_i - y_i`, with `sum_i delta_i = 0`. The reverse triangle inequality gives `|d_ij(X) - d_ij(Y)| <= |delta_i - delta_j|`, and `sum_{i,j} |delta_i - delta_j|^2 = 2N sum_i |delta_i|^2`. Hence `dRMSD^2 <= 2 RMSD^2` when dRMSD averages over all `N^2` ordered pairs and `dRMSD <= sqrt(2N/(N-1)) RMSD` when it averages over pairs `i != j`. Equality holds exactly when every `delta_i - delta_j` is parallel to both `x_i - x_j` and `y_i - y_j`, which for a generic configuration means a uniform dilation `Y = sX`. The constant is therefore sharp. Checked on 20,000 random pairs: the ratio never exceeded 1 and equalled 1 (to `6e-9`) for dilations. For small isotropic coordinate noise the first-order ratio is `dRMSD / RMSD ≈ sqrt(2/3) ≈ 0.82` (my estimate).

**Is the constant sharp?** (my analysis, checked numerically)

## C12. Cycle holonomy and frustration lower bounds for frame graphs

**Claim as checked.** For noisy relative transforms on a graph, the holonomy of a cycle lower-bounds the irreducible synchronisation error (`sum over cycle edges of errors >= d(holonomy, identity)` for a bi-invariant metric). In the abelian case the Hodge decomposition is exact and the minimum error is `h^T (C C^T)^{-1} h` in cycle space. What is the sharpest known lower bound in terms of cycle inconsistencies, and is there a non-abelian Hodge-type decomposition with rigorous curl/harmonic lower bounds?

## C13. Torsion error to Cartesian RMSD propagation

**Claim as checked.** Rotating a subset `B` of unit-mass atoms by `phi` about a bond axis while `A` stays fixed gives optimal-superposition cost `N RMSD^2 <= 2S - 2 sqrt(S^2 - 4 I_A I_B sin^2(phi/2)) <= 8 (I_A I_B / (I_A + I_B)) sin^2(phi/2)`, with `I_A`, `I_B` the moments of inertia about the axis and `S = I_A + I_B`; chained through the triangle inequality of the Kabsch pseudo-metric to `RMSD <= sum_k 2 sqrt(2 I_red,k) |sin(delta_k/2)| / sqrt(N)`.

**Reviewer's check.** Superposing by a rotation `psi` about the same axis costs `2 I_A (1 - cos psi) + 2 I_B (1 - cos(phi - psi))`, whose minimum over `psi` is the first expression; the second bound follows from `2S - 2 sqrt(S^2 - x) <= 2x/S`. On 20,000 random two-body instances the true Kabsch cost never exceeded the first expression (largest ratio `0.998`, median `0.69`), and it equalled it for coaxial symmetric tops. The small-angle limit is `I_red phi^2`, so the last bound is loose by a factor 2 in the square for small angles.

**Where a new result could sit.**

1. *Certification cost in torsion space with geometry-aware Lipschitz constants.* Combine the instance-dependent bound of Bachoc, Cesari & Gerchinovitz with the lever-arm constants of C13 (a Cartesian-Lipschitz energy has torsion-space Lipschitz constants proportional to `sqrt(2 I_red,k)`), to get explicit upper and lower bounds on the number of energy evaluations needed to certify a global minimum conformer, and show how decomposable structure escapes the `(Lip/eps)^m` barrier. I found nothing that connects certification complexity to chain geometry.
2. *A stability-chirality statement for distance-only models.* Using C11: a distance-only model can certify a structure only up to mirror image, the two candidates are exactly `2 sigma_r(X)` apart, and one pseudoscalar feature suffices to remove the ambiguity. Stated with constants, this would be a quantitative form of the chirality limitation that the expressivity literature states qualitatively.
