PROTOCOL OF THE CERTIFICATE BENCHMARK (experiments/eng/leafcert_bench)
Computational Research Division, 2026-11-26.  Protocol version 1.  The same protocol as data: config.py.

Written BEFORE any run on the test seeds.  Sections 1-8 are not changed afterwards; anything that had to
change is recorded in section 9, "Deviations", with the reason.

Subject: what it costs to PRODUCE and to CHECK a "shares at the leaves" certificate (the format of
src/metricchain_eng/leafcert) for the claim "min E = L" on a synthetic pairwise instance, against solving
the minimisation exactly without a certificate.  Producer: src/metricchain_eng/leafprod.


0. WHAT WAS KNOWN WHEN THIS WAS WRITTEN (so that the hypotheses can be judged)

The producer, its defaults and this protocol were developed on instances with the master seed string
"leafprod-dev" (indices 0-9 of some cells, including three trial runs of this benchmark's driver on the
families band3, complete, chain and grid) and on small random instances of the test-suite.  No instance
with the test master "leafcert-bench-v1" was solved before this file was written; one of them (grid, k = 3,
n = 25, index 0) was generated, not solved, to pin the generator's checksum in the test-suite.  From the
development runs (a few instances per cell, a machine shared with other jobs) the following was seen and
shaped the hypotheses and the design:
  * chains, trees and the attractive Potts grid were certified with one leaf at every size tried;
  * the search is fast (integer message passing in C: a few hundred to a few thousand nodes per second) and
    assembling plus checking the dense certificate took 3 to 10 times as long as the search whenever the
    tree had more than about a hundred leaves; the Python engine was the slowest part;
  * branching rule "strong" gave trees about half the size of "maxdeg"; a first rule ("probe": the site whose
    children keep the least total gap according to the node's own messages) gave trees 10 to 100 times
    larger on bands and grids and was removed; the leaf bound from the linear programme cost 10 to 1000
    times more per node and gave smaller trees on complete graphs in the first runs, about equal ones in
    the last trial (see H8);
  * a decoding step had to be added: without it the search did not find the minimiser of a chain of 100
    sites with 10 states and branched although the root bound was already the minimum;
  * the size caps and the rules R1-R3 below were set after the trial runs showed what a cell costs: with
    the first settings (general solvers on three instances of every ladder, the search without certificate
    on every instance) two families alone took more than 16 minutes on two CPUs;
  * CP-SAT with one worker and default parameters did not prove optimality within 20 s on a chain of 100
    sites with 10 states; with linearization_level = 2 it was 5 to 15 times faster on the development
    instances, and that setting is the baseline.  HiGHS as a mixed-integer solver was added as a second
    general solver because it was faster than CP-SAT on most development instances.


1. INSTANCES

Generator: metricchain_eng.leafprod.families.make(family, n, k, seed), seed = families.seed_of(MASTER,
family, k, n, index) = the first 8 bytes of sha256("MASTER|family|k=K|n=N|r=INDEX"), MASTER =
"leafcert-bench-v1", index = 0 .. 9.  Random numbers come from random.Random(seed) of the standard library
only.  Every instance's seed and a sha256 of its tables (families.digest) are stored with its results.

Tables: integers.  In the families chain, tree, band2, band3, grid, cubic, complete every entry of every
field H_i(a) and of every pair table J_ij(a, b) of an edge is uniform in {-1000, ..., 1000}, independently
(random signed couplings; no structure, hence frustration wherever the graph has cycles).  potts: the grid
with J_ij(a, b) = -w_ij [a = b], w_ij uniform in {1, ..., 1000}, and uniform fields in {-1000, ..., 1000}
(attractive; the easy case).  Graphs: chain = path; tree = random recursive tree with shuffled labels;
band2, band3 = pairs with |i - j| <= 2, 3; grid = rows x cols with open boundary, as square as n allows
(6 = 2x3, 12 = 3x4, 25 = 5x5, 49 = 7x7, 100 = 10x10); cubic = random 3-regular graph (pairing model,
loops and double edges rejected); complete = all pairs.

Alphabets: k in {2, 3, 5, 10} for all sites, and "mixed": every site draws its alphabet uniformly from
{2, 3, 5, 10}.

Sizes (a "ladder" is a family with an alphabet, climbed in increasing n):
  chain, tree, band2, band3:  6, 12, 25, 50, 100
  grid, potts:                6, 12, 25, 49, 100
  cubic:                      6, 12, 24, 50, 100
  complete:                   6, 8, 10, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36
8 families x 5 alphabets = 40 ladders; 10 instances per cell.


2. WHAT IS RUN ON AN INSTANCE (main pass)

(P) The producer: produce(instance, bound="mp", branching="strong", order="best", seed=0, partial=False),
    i.e. integer message passing for the leaves, strong branching, best-first search; target L = min E.
    Limits: 60 s for the search; at most 2,000,000 integers in the leaf certificates; at most 20,000,000
    checker operations (additions plus comparisons, the checker's own closed formula); at most 10^6 leaf
    boxes (not expected to bind).  The search stops as soon as the finished tree is known to exceed a cap
    (open nodes counted with what they need at least), so that a failure is cheap.  The two size caps are a
    budget decision: on this machine a certificate at the caps takes about 20 to 40 s to assemble and to
    check with both engines (the Python engine about 12 s for the operations alone), and the whole
    benchmark has two hours.  "Certified" means: produce() returned status "certified", which it does only
    after BOTH engines of the checker accepted the assembled object with identical counts.  A run that
    hits any limit is a failure and is counted as one.
    Measured: time of the search, of assembling, of check(obj, "c"), of check(obj, "python"), their sum;
    time of leafcert.c alone on its text encoding (best of 3); nodes, leaf boxes, distinct leaf
    certificates, depth; integers of the certificate and of the instance; checker operations; bytes of the
    compact JSON; the same counts for a hypothetical sparse variant of the format (formats.py); peak
    resident memory of the run (VmHWM, reset before the run).
(S) The same search without certificate: produce(..., certificate=False), 60 s, no size cap.  It visits the
    same nodes; nothing is stored, assembled or checked.  It isolates what the certificate itself costs,
    and it says how large the certificate would have been where the producer stopped at a cap.
(D) Dynamic programming (variable elimination, exact.eliminate) if the largest table of the greedy
    elimination order has at most 2^23 entries.  Exact; no proof left behind.
(B) Enumeration (exact.brute_force) if the domain has at most 2^24 states.  Ground truth.
(C) OR-tools CP-SAT 9.15, one worker, linearization_level = 2, a Boolean per (site, state) and per
    (edge, state, state) tied by the equalities of the pairwise relaxation; 60 s.  It proves optimality
    internally and leaves no proof that our checker can verify.
(M) HiGHS as a mixed-integer solver (scipy.optimize.milp) on the same relaxation with integral site
    marginals, relative gap 0; 60 s.  Same remark.
(R) The pairwise relaxation at the root (scipy linprog, HiGHS dual simplex, 20 s): its value, for the gap
    "optimum minus relaxation".

Rules that keep the benchmark within its time (all fixed here, none decided after seeing results):
  R1  (C) and (M) are run on the instances 0 and 1 of a cell only, and only on the ladders with k in {2, 3}
      and on the ladders with k = 5 of chain, grid and complete.  A general solver leaves a ladder after its
      first time-limit hit.  (Development: for k = 10 they need seconds per instance already at n = 6.)
  R2  (S) is run on every instance the producer certified and on the first three instances of a cell that
      it did not certify.  It stops within a cell after two time-limit hits (the remaining instances of the
      cell are not run with it) and then leaves the ladder; it also leaves the ladder if it solved fewer
      than half of the instances it was run on.
  R3  (P) is run on all 10 instances of a cell.  It leaves the ladder after a cell in which it certified
      fewer than 5 of 10.  Cells of the ladder after that are "extension cells": instances 0-2 only, the
      baselines that are still on the ladder, no producer.
  R4  A ladder ends when neither (P) nor (S) is on it, or at its last size.
  R5  Budget rule.  Cells are scheduled level by level (all ladders at their first size, then all at their
      second, ...; within a level in the order chain, tree, potts, grid, cubic, band2, band3, complete and
      k = 2, 3, 5, 10, mixed), on two worker processes.  No new cell of the main pass is started after 64
      minutes of wall time; a started cell is finished.  Cells not started are reported as "not run
      (budget)", never as failures or successes.
  R6  Every instance that was run is reported.  No instance, cell or solver result is dropped.

Consistency: all exact values obtained for an instance (certified L, (S), (D), (B), (C), (M)) must be equal,
and no solver may report a state below them.  Any disagreement is reported as an error of the benchmark.


3. COMPARISON PASS (leaf-bound methods and branching rules), 20 minutes, same budget rule

Searches without certificate on instances of the main pass (same seeds), 60 s each; a configuration stops in
a cell after two time-limit hits.
  Branching rules, bound "mp", all 10 instances: strong (main), maxdeg, margin, maxdom, and strong with
  share=False, on the cells
      band3 k=3 n=50, band3 k=5 n=50, band2 k=5 n=50, grid k=5 n=49, cubic k=5 n=50, complete k=2 n=20,
      complete k=3 n=12, complete k=5 n=10, complete k=10 n=8, complete mixed n=12, grid mixed n=49,
      band3 k=2 n=50, band2 k=3 n=50, grid k=3 n=49, grid k=10 n=49, cubic k=3 n=50, potts k=5 n=49.
  Leaf-bound methods, instances 0-4: mp/strong, lp/strong (the linear programme, rounded), hybrid/strong,
  lp/frac, on the cells (smaller, because one linear programme per node is slow)
      complete k=2 n=20, complete k=3 n=12, complete k=5 n=8, band3 k=3 n=25, band3 k=5 n=25,
      band2 k=5 n=25, grid k=5 n=25, grid k=10 n=25, cubic k=5 n=24.
  On the instances 0 and 1 of these nine cells lp/strong and hybrid/strong are also run as producers (same
  caps), so that certificates from rounded linear programmes are built and checked, not only counted.
Order of the cells: the nine bound cells first, then the remaining rule cells as listed.


4. SECOND PASS (600 s), 16 minutes, same budget rule

For each entry of the list below, in this order: the first cell of the ladder in which the solver missed an
instance in the main pass, and the first two instances it missed there; that solver alone, 600 s; the
producer with the caps raised five-fold (10,000,000 integers, 100,000,000 operations).
    complete k=2: search without certificate; complete k=2: producer; band3 k=3: producer;
    complete k=2: MILP; complete k=2: CP-SAT; complete k=5: producer; complete k=5: search; grid k=5: producer.
The purpose is to see whether ten times the time (and five times the certificate) moves the frontier.


5. MACHINE AND LIMITS

One laptop CPU (4 cores, 8 threads), shared with other jobs of the project during the runs: timings are
noisy and are reported as medians with ranges; the load average is recorded at the start and the end of
each pass.  Two worker processes, each pinned to one logical CPU (5 and 7, different cores), one thread
each (OMP/BLAS thread variables set to 1; CP-SAT num_workers = 1), address space limited to 3 GiB per
worker; with the caps a worker is expected to stay below 1 GiB resident.  Wall time: 64 + 20 + 16 minutes
plus the cells in progress when a budget ends; target at most two hours in all.

Reproducibility: with the caps, whether an instance is certified does not depend on timing unless the 60 s
search limit is hit (expected: never for the producer).  Certificates are deterministic; their sha256 is
stored.


6. HYPOTHESES (stated before the runs; each is checked by make_tables.py, section "numbers for the hypotheses")

H1  Forests.  Every chain and tree instance (500) is certified with exactly one leaf box.  At n = 100 the
    search is the smaller part of the producer's time (assembling and checking take more than half), the
    producer needs less than 5 s per instance even for k = 10, and dynamic programming is at least 10 times
    faster than producing and checking (median).
H2  Potts grid.  k = 2: all 50 instances with one leaf.  k >= 3 and mixed: all instances certified, and at
    least 8 of 10 instances of every cell with one leaf.
H3  Sparse frustrated families (grid, cubic, band2, band3).
    (a) every instance with n <= 12 is certified;
    (b) the median number of leaf boxes does not decrease with n in any ladder, and in band3 (k >= 3) and
        band2 (k >= 5) it grows by more than a factor 4 from n = 25 to n = 50;
    (c) the producer leaves the ladder (fewer than 5 of 10 certified) at n = 100 or earlier in band3 with
        k = 3, 5, 10, in band2 with k = 5, 10 and in grid with k = 10; it certifies at least 8 of 10 at
        n = 100 in grid k = 2, cubic k = 2 and cubic k = 3;
    (d) where the producer fails in these families the cause is a size cap, not the 60 s limit, and the
        search without certificate solves the same instance within 60 s.
H4  Complete graphs.
    (a) the relaxation is far from tight: the relative gap (optimum - relaxation) / |optimum| exceeds 20 %
        for every instance with k = 2 and n >= 16;
    (b) the median number of leaf boxes grows geometrically with n: by a factor between 1.2 and 1.6 per
        added site for k = 2, and by more than 1.6 per site for k = 5;
    (c) the producer leaves the ladder at n = 32 for k = 2 (at least 5 of 10 certified at n = 28), at n = 16
        for k = 5, and at n = 10 or n = 12 for k = 10;
    (d) at the size where the producer leaves, the search without certificate still solves at least half
        of the instances within 60 s.
H5  Cost of the certificate.  For certified instances with more than 100 leaf certificates, assembling and
    checking take longer than the search in at least 90 % of the instances and at least twice as long in
    the median, and the Python engine takes more than half of the time after the search (median).
H6  Checking.  leafcert.c alone checks every certificate of the benchmark in less than 1 s.  In
    check(obj, "c") more than 80 % of the time is strict loading and encoding on the Python side, not
    leafcert.c (median over certificates with at least 10^5 operations).  Per operation the Python engine is
    20 to 100 times slower than leafcert.c.
H7  Density (arithmetic, stated in advance).  At n = 100 a dense leaf certificate of a chain with k = 10 has
    104,050 integers against 2,179 in a sparse variant (48 times); on the 10 x 10 grid with k = 10, 104,050
    against 3,880 (27 times); on complete graphs the two are equal.  Consequence expected in the data: at
    n = 100, at least 80 % of the producer's failures are stops at the integer cap while the search itself
    completes.
H8  Leaf-bound methods.  With the same branching rule the two leaf bounds give trees of similar size: the
    median ratio of leaf boxes lp/mp lies between 0.5 and 2 in every one of the nine cells (the last trial
    on development seeds showed ratios of 1.0 to 1.15 on complete graphs; an earlier impression that the
    programme gives clearly smaller trees there did not survive it).  The lp search is slower than the mp
    search in every cell, at least 5 times in the median.  hybrid/strong has tree sizes within 20 % of
    lp/strong (median) at a time between the two.  The value lost by rounding the programme's multipliers
    at the root is below 20 units in the median in every cell.
H9  Branching rules.  "strong" has the smallest median number of leaf boxes in at least 13 of the 17 cells,
    at no more than 3 times the search time of "maxdeg" (median).  "maxdom" produces exactly the trees of
    "maxdeg" on uniform alphabets (true by construction: a consistency check) and trees at least 1.5 times
    larger on the two mixed cells (median).
H10 Baselines.
    (a) dynamic programming applies to every chain, tree, band2 and band3 instance and is the fastest exact
        method on at least 90 % of those with n >= 50;
    (b) on complete graphs with n >= 16 the search without certificate is faster than MILP and than CP-SAT
        in the median, in every cell where they are run;
    (c) CP-SAT leaves the complete ladders (first 60 s hit) at a smaller n than the search does;
    (d) for certified band2 and band3 instances that needed branching, producing and checking takes at
        least 10 times as long as the fastest exact baseline (median): an order of magnitude or more.
H11 Shared leaves.  For k = 2 the number of leaf certificates equals the number of leaf boxes in every
    instance.  For k = 10 sharing saves between 5 % and 30 % of the leaf certificates (distinct certificates
    against leaf boxes, summed over the certified instances with at least two leaf boxes): a modest saving.
H12 Exploratory, no threshold: how often the relaxation is tight at the root (LP value = optimum) while the
    producer still needs more than one leaf -- integer messages at zero margin, or the ascent stalling
    below the programme's value.
H13 Memory.  No producer run exceeds 1 GiB of resident memory.

Expected failure modes of the producer, to be counted: integer cap; operation cap; 60 s search limit
(expected: none); a certificate rejected by the checker (expected: none; would be a defect); inconsistent
exact values (expected: none).


7. ANALYSIS

Per cell: number certified out of 10 and the reasons of the failures; median and range (minimum-maximum)
of leaf boxes, leaf certificates, depth, integers, bytes, and of the times (producer: over the certified
instances; baselines: over the instances they solved, with the count solved / attempted).  Ratios are
computed per instance and summarised by median and range.  Growth factors per site are computed from the
median leaf boxes of the search without certificate at the first and the last size of a ladder at which
it solved all instances and the median exceeds 1 (descriptive, two points; no model is fitted).  No
significance tests: the statements are about counts and medians of 10 instances per cell, and the cell
tables give the ranges.  Exact (integer arithmetic, accepted by the checker): every "certified", every
count of boxes, certificates, integers and operations, every agreement of exact values.  Measured: every
time and memory figure.  Conjectured: anything said beyond the sizes that were run.

Tables: python experiments/eng/leafcert_bench/make_tables.py regenerates every table of the report from
results/main, results/compare, results/long and writes results/tables.txt; --verify compares.

9. DEVIATIONS AND ADDITIONS (written after the runs, 2026-11-27)

Sections 0-8 are as they were before the first run on the test seeds (sha256 of this file at that time:
40432dfaf78bae04...; of config.py: c870885c2f57e152..., unchanged since).  No rule was changed and no budget
was reached:
  main pass        2047 s of wall time (budget for starting cells: 3840 s): 211 cells, i.e. every cell that
                   the rules R1-R4 allowed; none was cut by the budget rule R5.
  comparison pass   526 s (budget 1200 s): all 24 cells.
  second pass       175 s (budget  960 s): all 10 tasks that the rule of section 4 produced (for four of
                   its eight entries the solver had missed one instance or none in the main pass).
  The three passes together: 2748 s, started 2026-11-26 23:17 UTC, finished 2026-11-27 00:03 UTC.

Additions after the freeze (neither changes a stored number of the three passes):
  A1  integrality_probe.py and results/exploratory_integrality.json: an exploratory look at H12 (why does
      the producer branch when the relaxation is tight at the root?).  Not a test stated in advance.
  A2  make_tables.py was extended after the first results had been seen: the compact tables per family,
      the column "ended by" of the overview, the table of the exploratory probe, and the way the numbers
      for H3-H13 are printed.  It only reads the stored results.

  A3  A spot re-run after the passes, nothing stored: one randomly chosen instance of each of the 199
      cells with producer runs was regenerated and produced again with the settings of section 2.  The
      checksum of every instance, the status of every run (199 of 199) and the sha256 of every certificate
      (186 of 186 certified) were the same as in the main pass.

What did not go as this protocol assumed:
  B1  H7 expected the integer cap to be the usual stop at n = 100; the operation cap was (31 of 48 stops).
      Both caps were fixed in advance; the expectation was wrong, not the rule.
  B2  Because of rule R2, "the search without certificate solves the instance" (H3d, H4d, H7) could be
      checked only on the first three uncertified instances of a cell (45 of the 101 failures in the sparse
      frustrated families).
  B3  The machine was less loaded than during development (load average 7.3 at the start of the main
      pass and 2.8 at its end; 2 to 3 during the other two passes; 9 to 14 during the trial runs).  The
      passes therefore ended far inside their budgets, and times are not comparable with the trial runs.
  B4  An operator error without effect on the data: a shell command queued to start the comparison pass
      after the main pass waited on a condition that could never become true and was killed before it had
      started anything; a corrected command then ran the comparison pass, the second pass and the probe in
      the order of section 8.
