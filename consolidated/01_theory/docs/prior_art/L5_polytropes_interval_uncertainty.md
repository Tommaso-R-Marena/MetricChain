# L5 prior-art check: Lipschitz classes as polytropes, interval/difference-constraint uncertainty, robust Gibbs measures

Literature check performed on 2026-11-24 for the MetricChain program. Setting of the claims: `X` finite; `d : X x X -> R` with `d(x,x) = 0` and the triangle inequality, possibly asymmetric (the "envelope"); `Lip(d) = { g : X -> R : g(x) - g(y) <= d(x,y) for all x, y }`; a known function `f`; an unknown `g in Lip(d)`, possibly with evaluations `g(a) in [lo_a, hi_a]` at some points; `E = f + g`; Gibbs weights `exp(-beta E)`. Covers candidate claims P1-P9.

## Reading guide

- **Verdicts.** `KNOWN` (a citation states it), `KNOWN-IN-SPIRIT` (an immediate corollary or routine combination of cited results, or stated in a neighbouring field under another name), `PARTIALLY KNOWN` (substantial special cases or the structural half are in print, the stated generality is not), `NO MATCH FOUND` (the listed searches did not surface it; this is not a proof of novelty).
- **Evidence tags.** `[V]` = the bibliographic record was confirmed online in this session **and** the quoted statement, equation or theorem was read in the paper's own text (arXiv PDF, author or institutional copy, open proceedings). `[V-abs]` = record confirmed, but only the abstract was readable (publisher, Crossref, OpenAlex or Semantic Scholar metadata), so theorem numbers and body content are not checked. `[U]` = not verified in this session: either only the bibliographic record was confirmed and the content attributed to it is my recollection, or the item is quoted through another paper that cites it. Each `[U]` entry says which.
- **Sources of verification.** arXiv (PDFs and API), Crossref, OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar metadata, author and institutional copies (UCLA, LAMSADE, Warwick CRiSM, ICTP Cluj, INS Bonn, IRIT, NeurIPS proceedings). DOIs or arXiv identifiers are given so every item can be re-checked.
- **How texts were read.** PDFs were text-extracted and the quoted passages read directly. No statement below rests on an automated page summary, except where an entry says "search summary only" (those are tagged `[U]`).
- **Reviewer's derivations and numerics.** Several short arguments are mine and are marked as such. They show why a claim is or is not a corollary of the cited literature. They have not been refereed. Those for P2, P3, P5, P6, P7 and P9 were sanity-checked by brute force (linear programs and vertex enumeration on random instances with 3 to 7 points) with throwaway scripts that are not part of the repository; counts are in section 10.
- **Notation.** `D*` is the shortest-path closure (min-plus Kleene star) of a weight matrix; for an envelope, `d = d*`. `U_A(x) = min_a [hi_a + d(x,a)]` and `L_A(y) = max_a [lo_a - d(a,y)]` are the upper and lower McShane envelopes of the data. `pi` is the reference Gibbs measure proportional to `exp(-beta f)`.
- **Limits of this check** are listed in section 11. The most important: several publishers blocked automated access (McShane 1934, the two Annals of Statistics papers, Kasperski's book, Rubinov's book, all ScienceDirect full texts), so those items are abstract-level or `[U]`; Russian-language originals were not searched; the arXiv API handles phrase queries poorly, so the arXiv sweeps are less reliable than they look.

## P1. Identification of `Lip(d)`

**Claim as checked.** `Lip(d)` modulo constants is simultaneously (i) a system of difference constraints, feasible iff there is no negative cycle (Bellman-Ford); (ii) a Simple Temporal Network, whose minimal network is the all-pairs shortest-path matrix and which is decomposable; (iii) a polytrope = alcoved polytope of type A = tropical span of the columns of a Kleene star; (iv) an L-natural-convex polyhedron; (v) the Lipschitz polytope of a finite metric space, polar to the Kantorovich-Rubinstein ("fundamental") polytope. The brief also asks for what is known about the vertices and about asymmetric versions.

**Vertices of the Lipschitz polytope.**

- *Characterisation.* Gordon-Petrov `[V]`: a face of `KR(X)` is cut out by some `f_0 in LIP(X)`, and its graph `D(alpha)` has the edge `x -> y` "iff `f_0(x) - f_0(y) = rho(x,y)`". Lemma 1: "Dimension of a face alpha equals `n - c`, where `c` is the number of connected components" of that graph (`|X| = n+1`). Hence `f_0` is a vertex of `LIP(X)` iff its graph of tight pairs is connected. Theorem 3: a directed graph is the tight graph of some 1-Lipschitz function iff it satisfies the cyclic-monotonicity inequality `sum rho(x_i, y_i) <= sum rho(x_i, y_{i+1})`. Corollary 1: for a strict metric every `(delta_x - delta_y)/rho` is a vertex of `KR`, and each vertex of a tight graph has in-degree or out-degree zero.
- *Count.* Gordon-Petrov Theorem 1 `[V]`: for a generic metric (strict triangle inequalities and `KR` simplicial) on `n+1` points the number of `(n-m)`-dimensional faces of `LIP(X)` is `(n+m)! / (m! m! (n-m)!)`; with `m = n` this gives `binom(2n, n)` vertices. This equals the general upper bound for polytropes (item 3, Proposition 6; item 5, Proposition 19). For the discrete metric on `[n]` the vertices are the `2^n - 2` vectors `e_I`, `I` a proper nonempty subset (Çelik et al., Lemma 5 `[V]`). For graph metrics, "the facets of the Lipschitz polytope `P_d` arising from a graph G correspond to the edges of G" (Çelik et al., eq. (4.1) `[V]`), and the dual is the symmetric edge polytope: A. D'Alì, E. Delucchi, M. Michałek, "Many faces of symmetric edge polytopes", Electron. J. Combin. 29(3) (2022), [doi:10.37236/10387](https://doi.org/10.37236/10387), [arXiv:1910.05193](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05193) `[V-abs]`. For tree-like metrics: E. Delucchi, L. Hoessly, "Fundamental polytopes of metric trees via parallel connections of matroids", European J. Combin. (2020), [doi:10.1016/j.ejc.2020.103098](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2020.103098), [arXiv:1612.05534](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.05534) `[V-abs]`: "explicit formulas for the face numbers of fundamental polytopes and Lipschitz polytopes of all tree-like metrics".
- *Reviewer's numerics.* Strict generic envelopes on 4 and 5 points gave 20 and 70 vertices in every trial, symmetric or not (230 instances each way). Envelopes that are shortest-path closures of random weights are usually not strict and gave anything from 4 to 20 vertices on 4 points. Site-additive (Hamming-type) envelopes are graph metrics, so they are in the non-generic regime where the count depends on the instance.
- *Enumeration.* Joswig-Loho `[V]` note that "potentials and weighted digraph polyhedra starred prominently in the work of Khachiyan and al. [19] on hardness results in the context of vertex enumeration". Their [19] is L. Khachiyan, E. Boros, K. Borys, K. Elbassioni, V. Gurvich, "Generating all vertices of a polyhedron is hard", Discrete Comput. Geom. 39(1-3):174-190 (2008) (details as given in the reference list of Joswig-Loho); that paper was not read (`[U]`), so which polyhedra its hardness result covers is not checked here.
- *Infinite metric spaces.* Extreme points of the unit ball of Lipschitz function spaces are a classical subject (Roy 1968, Rao-Roy 1970, Cobzaş 1989, Farmer 1994, as listed in the abstract of Mustăţa 2002 below). Those papers were not read (`[U]`).

## P2. McShane-Whitney as tropical projection and STN decomposability

**Claim as checked.** The lower and upper McShane extensions are the least and greatest solutions of the constraint system. Given the data, the set of possible values of `g(x) - g(y)` is exactly `[-D_A(y,x), D_A(x,y)]` with `D_A(x,y) = min(d(x,y), U_A(x) - L_A(y))`, a rank-one update of the Kleene star.

**Reviewer's derivation (the claim is correct).** Add a node `0` with `g(0) = 0`. The data are the constraints `g(a) - g(0) <= hi_a` and `g(0) - g(a) <= -lo_a`. Shortest paths in the augmented graph give `x -> 0` of length `U_A(x)`, `0 -> y` of length `-L_A(y)`, and `x -> y` of length `min(d(x,y), U_A(x) - L_A(y))`, provided there is no negative cycle, that is, `lo_a <= hi_a` and `lo_a - hi_b <= d(a,b)` for all anchors. In min-plus notation `D_A = d ⊕ (U ⊗ (-L)^T)`, with `⊕` the entrywise minimum and `U ⊗ (-L)^T` the matrix of sums `U(x) - L(y)`: one tropical rank-one term for all anchors together. Each single added constraint (an edge `a -> b` of weight `w`) changes the closure by the rank-one term `d(x,a) + w + d(b,y)`. Exactness of the interval is minimality of the closed network (source 1 or 2 below). The check on 200 random instances with interval anchors (5,212 linear programs) found no discrepancy.

## P3. Possibly and necessarily optimal solutions under polytrope uncertainty

**Claim as checked.** For `E = f + g`, `g in Lip(d)`: (a) `x` minimises `E` for some `g` iff `f(x) <= f(y) + d(y,x)` for all `y`; (b) `x` minimises `E` for every `g` iff `d(x,y) <= f(y) - f(x)` for all `y`; (c) the maximal regret of `x` is `max_y [f(x) - f(y) + d(x,y)]`.

**Reviewer's derivation (correct; where it sits).** `E` ranges over `Pol(c)` with `c(x,y) = d(x,y) + f(x) - f(y)`, a closed matrix that may have negative entries. By minimality `E(x) - E(y)` ranges over `[-c(y,x), c(x,y)]`, and by decomposability the differences to a fixed `x` are attained simultaneously, at `g = d(., x)` for the lower ends and `g = -d(x, .)` for the upper ends. So (a) is "row `x` of `c^T` is nonnegative", (b) is "row `x` of `c` is nonpositive", (c) is "the largest entry of row `x` of `c`". In temporal-network language: `x` can be the earliest event, must be the earliest event, and its largest possible lateness. Checks on 60 random instances (717 comparisons with linear programs or vertex enumeration): no discrepancy.

**The classical special case: interval element costs.** Let solutions be subsets of a ground set, `f(x) = sum_{e in x} c0_e`, and let each cost vary in `[c_e^-, c_e^+]`. The induced envelope is `d_int(x,y) = sum_{e in x\y} (c_e^+ - c0_e) + sum_{e in y\x} (c0_e - c_e^-)`, an asymmetric weighted Hamming distance. The box of additive perturbations is a proper subset of `Lip(d_int)` with the same pairwise suprema, attained simultaneously at the scenario "elements of `x` at one bound, all others at the other bound". Therefore (a)-(c) for the box and for `Lip(d_int)` coincide, and they are the following known statements.

## P5. Exact range of a Gibbs probability at finite temperature

**Claim as checked.** `sup_{g in Lip(d)} p_g(x) = 1 / sum_y exp(-beta (f(y) - f(x) + d(y,x)))`, attained by `g = d(., x)` for every `beta`; `inf = 1 / sum_y exp(-beta (f(y) - f(x) - d(x,y)))`.

**Reviewer's derivation (correct).** `p_g(x) = 1 / sum_y exp(-beta (f(y) - f(x) + g(y) - g(x)))` is increasing in each difference `g(y) - g(x)`, and `-d(x,y) <= g(y) - g(x) <= d(y,x)`. All upper ends are attained together by `g = d(., x)` and all lower ends by `g = -d(x, .)`; both lie in `Lip(d)` (Cobzaş, Proposition 7.4). Checked on 60 random instances against vertex enumeration and random interior points (956 comparisons): no discrepancy.

## P6. Log-Lipschitz density classes are polyhedral; robust expectations are linear programs

**Claim as checked.** In `w = exp(-beta g)` the class is the cone `{w > 0 : w(y) <= exp(beta d(x,y)) w(x)}`. The range of a Boltzmann average or of a ratio of partition functions over the class is a linear-fractional program, hence a linear program, with optima at vertices of the Lipschitz polytope. Question: is a class with pairwise log-density-ratio bounds governed by a metric studied, and is the LP/vertex structure stated?

**Reviewer's derivation (correct).** A tight constraint in `w` is the same pair as a tight constraint in `g`. An extreme ray of the cone needs tight pairs forming a connected graph, as does a vertex of `Lip(d)` modulo constants (P1). So `exp(-beta .)` maps vertices of the Lipschitz polytope bijectively to extreme rays of the cone. A ratio of two linear forms over a cone, with positive denominator, is maximised on an extreme ray. On 60 random instances the Charnes-Cooper linear program and the maximum over enumerated vertices agreed (120 comparisons).

## P7. Marginalisation

**Claim as checked.** For a product reference measure and a site-additive envelope, the range of the expectation of a single-site observable over the class equals the range over the single-site class with that site's constant. For correlated references the effective constant is inflated by an exponential moment of a coupling distance.

**Reviewer's derivation (first half correct, and more is true).** Let `pi` be a product measure, `d(x,y) = sum_i d_i(x_i,y_i)` with `d_i >= 0`, `w = exp(-beta g)`. The site-1 marginal has density `w1(a) = E_{pi_{-1}}[w(a, Z)]` against `pi_1`, and `w(a,z) <= exp(beta d_1(b,a)) w(b,z)` for every `z`, so `w1` satisfies the single-site constraints. Conversely every single-site weight extends as a function of the first coordinate. So the set of site-1 marginals of the joint class is exactly the single-site class, and all ranges agree. The same holds for a block of sites. Checked on 60 random two-site instances (120 linear programs): no discrepancy. With a correlated reference the supremum of a single-site probability over the joint class exceeded the single-site value in 60 of 60 random instances.

## P8. Maslov dequantisation

**Claim as checked.** The zero-temperature limit of the robust Gibbs-measure bounds gives the possibly/necessarily-optimal criteria.

**Reviewer's derivation.** From P5, `-(1/beta) log sup_g p_g(x) -> max(0, max_y [f(x) - f(y) - d(y,x)])`, the smallest regret `x` can have over the class, which vanishes iff `x` is possibly optimal. `-(1/beta) log inf_g p_g(x) -> max(0, max_y [f(x) - f(y) + d(x,y)])`, the maximal regret of P3, which vanishes iff `x` is necessarily optimal. The error at finite `beta` is at most `(log |X|)/beta`. The criteria themselves do not need the limit: by De Bock et al., Theorem 1, the worst-case mode test is the same inequality at every `beta` (P3 item 6).

**Reviewer's remarks (unrefereed).**
- The anchored component is the mixed difference: `g_u = prod_{j in u} (I - P_j) g`, where `P_j` freezes coordinate `j` at the anchor. So "order at most `m`" is "all anchored mixed differences of order `m+1` vanish", and the exactness statement is a tautology once this is written. Checked on random functions of order at most 2 on `3^4` configurations (480 reconstructions): exact.
- The remainder at a configuration at Hamming distance `r` from the anchor is a sum over the subsets of size greater than `m` of the `r` displaced sites. A uniform bound `eps_k` on order-`k` anchored mixed differences gives `|R_m(x)| <= sum_{k=m+1}^{r} binom(r,k) eps_k`, and a bounded-difference constant at one site of at most `2 sum_{k>=m+1} binom(r-1, k-1) eps_k`, where `r` counts the displaced sites including the changed one (the factor 2 disappears when one of the two values at that site is the anchor value). The envelope of the remainder therefore grows with the distance from the anchor. The class is "m-body plus a remainder that is Lipschitz for a distance-dependent envelope", unless the `eps_k` decay fast enough to beat the binomial factors.

**What was not found.** The anchored decomposition with a certified remainder used as an uncertainty class for pruning, for partition-function bounds or for robust Gibbs measures. A discrete Taylor formula with remainder stated for functions on products of finite sets in the literature searched (a search on anchored ANOVA, mixed differences and Möbius inversion returned the continuous theory only).

**Verdict.** Exact part: KNOWN. Remainder control: KNOWN-IN-SPIRIT. Use as a certified uncertainty class: NO MATCH FOUND. **Plain classification:** textbook (anchored ANOVA / cut-HDMR, Möbius inversion); the remainder bound is a routine consequence; the application is apparently new. **Confidence:** medium-high for the first two, medium for the gap.

**How to sharpen it into something new.**
1. Write the envelope of the remainder explicitly as a function of the Hamming distance to the anchor and prove that it is tight for the class "anchored mixed differences of order `k` bounded by `eps_k`". Then P2-P7 apply with this envelope.
2. Several anchors: the envelope is small only near an anchor. Once the low-order parts are evaluated, the remainders for two anchors differ by a known function, so the multi-anchor class is an intersection of polytropes shifted by known functions. A shift by `h` replaces `d(x,y)` by `d(x,y) + h(x) - h(y)`, so the intersection is again a polytrope, with envelope the closure of the entrywise minimum. Optimal anchor placement is a covering problem of the kind in P4.
3. A physical instance with a proved, not fitted, remainder. Thomas-Chen-Ortner give exponential convergence of body-ordered approximations for tight-binding models; turning such a rate into the constants `eps_k` of a certified envelope was not found.

## 12. Where novelty most plausibly sits

- Not in P1, P2, P3 or P8 as stated. Each is textbook, a specialisation of a named theorem, or a one-line corollary.
- In P6 with P5 and P7: the finite metric-governed class of Gibbs measures as an object of robust inference, positioned as the generalisation of Walley's bounded derivative model and of density-ratio classes, with exact point-probability ranges, exact block-marginal ranges for product references, and a sharp statement for correlated references.
- In P4: certificate size for finite quasi-metric spaces with a known part (hardness, packing bounds, comparison with Piyavskii-Shubert), which the Lipschitz-optimisation literature has only for intervals and compact subsets of `R^d`.
- In P3: robust combinatorial optimisation with Lipschitz-in-solution-space uncertainty as a model, with the interval-data theory as its additive special case (the 2-approximation transfers in three lines; hardness and structured cases are open).
- In P9: a certified, distance-dependent envelope for the remainder of an anchored expansion, with several anchors, feeding the machinery above.
- Throughout: succinct instances (product spaces, site-additive envelopes), where the classical all-pairs objects are implicit and the questions become complexity questions.
