Registry entry Q-11.  Committed before the launch; afterwards only the section

## What is evaluated

The Lipschitz class of the cube with **site-dependent slopes**: sites of two types,
`n_H` with slope `u_H` and `n_L` with slope `u_L < u_H`; `|ψ(x) − ψ(x ⊕ e_i)| ≤ u_i`; uniform
reference measure; certified accuracy `0.8715`, `δ = 0.05` (the top level of X-011).

Four quantities per cell, in nats per site (`src/metricchain_eng/twolevel/hetero.py`, whose
header states what each rests on):

1. the **asymptotic exponent** `h = max_t Σ_j λ_j F_j(t)` of the theory report's §11.26 (one
   slope `t` for all site types), with `F_j` the Rényi-capacity form for a binary symmetric
   site;
2. the **exact lower bound** at this `n` for every algorithm: the smallest weighted dip
   whose excess exceeds the accuracy (the argument of Theorem 11.25(a) with weighted
   balls);
3. the certified bound of the **homogeneous algorithm with slope `u_H`** (admissible by
   inclusion), family "wide", at most three nested levels;
4. the certified bound of **type-aligned nesting**: Algorithm 2L⁺ with every outer block
   made of sites of one type, the constants of Theorem K1 with the substitutions listed in
   the module's header — **a transfer of the proof by this division, not refereed** — and
   servers chosen by certified cost; at most three nested levels.

Grid: `(u_H, u_L) ∈ {(2, ½), (1, ½), (2, 1), (4, 1), (4, ¼)}`; fraction of heavy sites
`0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9`; `n ∈ {1000, 4000, 16 000}`: 75 cells.

Independent of the job: literal runs of the algorithm with those constants on cubes of
11–16 sites with real fluctuations and explicit energy tables (X-014b,
`hetero_literal.py`), which test item 4's transfer where exact truth is available.

## What was already seen when this was written

Five cells, run locally while the code was written: `(2, ½)` with half heavy sites at
`n = 2000, 4000`; `(1, ½)` with half at `n = 2000`; `(2, ½)` with a quarter heavy at `n = 2000`
and `4000`.  In all five the type-aligned bound was below the homogeneous one (for example
`0.069` against `0.161` nats per site with three nested levels at `n = 4000`, a quarter
heavy; lower bound `0.031`).  The hypotheses below were written knowing these five.

## Hypotheses

* **H1.**  In every cell and for two and three nested levels the type-aligned bound is
  below the homogeneous one, and their ratio falls as the fraction of heavy sites falls.
  Not true by construction: the type-aligned family contains no mixed outer block.
  *Refuted by* a cell in which the homogeneous bound is lower.
* **H2.**  The exact lower bound per site exceeds `h` in every cell and the excess shrinks
  from `n = 1000` to `16 000`.
* **H3.**  At `n = 16 000` the best type-aligned bound is between `1.5` and `3` times the lower
  bound in every cell (the uniform cube at `u = 1`: `1.73` with four levels).
* **H4.**  The exponent `h` of a mixed chain differs from the average of the one-type
  exponents by less than one per cent in every cell (for binary symmetric sites the
  optimal `t` hardly depends on the slope) — so the *non-additivity* that §11.26 proves
  is numerically irrelevant for two-state sites, unlike the mixtures of alphabets
  reported there.

## What this cannot show

The bounds of item 4 are certified only if the transfer of Theorem K1 is right; the
literal runs test it on small cubes, they do not prove it.  Two site types, binary
sites, one accuracy; a finite family of designs.  Nothing is run on instances (no
worst-case search): these are bounds, not costs.

One pass.

## Addendum of 2026-11-26, before job M-022: a continuous profile of slopes, binned (X-014c)

`n` sites with slopes equally spaced on `[½, 2]` — every site its own slope, the case the
theory division's assumption audit lists as unclassified ("upper bounds for genuinely
heterogeneous sites").  Type-aligned nesting needs blocks of one slope, so the slopes are
binned into `m` classes of equal size and every site is given the largest slope of its
class; the binned class contains the original one, so its certified bound is a bound for
the original.  `m = 1` is the homogeneous algorithm.  Seen before the job (local, at most
three levels): at `n = 16 000` the bound per site is `0.155, 0.121, 0.126, 0.113, 0.163` for
`m = 1, 2, 3, 4, 6` — not monotone in `m`, because with three levels a fine binning leaves a
mixed innermost block.

**H5.**  With up to five levels the bound for `m = 4` and for `m = 6` is below the bound for
`m = 2`, at both sizes: finer binning pays once the nesting is deep enough to use it.
**H6.**  No binning brings the certified bound below twice the profile's asymptotic
exponent (`0.0467` per site) at `n = 16 000`.
